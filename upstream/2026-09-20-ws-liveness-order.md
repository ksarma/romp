Upstream's copy of tests/test_ws_liveness.py carries the same harness race: test_c3 clears pingAt (its stand-in for the handler's return from a dispatch) and asserts None at once, while the second beat's ping, queued two lines earlier, may still be on its way to the socket, where the sender stamps pingAt with the clock. Both orders are valid kernel states, so the kernel is unchanged: the test now waits for that ping to reach the peer before it clears, the wait is guarded by a case that runs the same body under the forced late order and reds when the wait is deleted, the late order is run on purpose by its own case, and the forcing instrument's release of a held sender on exit is exercised by a committed case at a bound that can show it, whose claim is the hold's own record: the hold, entered before or after the exit, returned released with the exit already run, not expired, and the case makes no claim that the thread was blocked in the wait or had reached the hold before the exit. The judge's read order (inRead before pingAt in _keepalive_all) was already pinned before this change by tests/test_view_deltas.py, TwoThreadsOneClient.test_c_the_beat_reads_the_read_state_before_the_ping_stamp (in the fork since 2026-09-04 and in the project), which reds on the swap, so this change adds no pin on it; the two cases the first cut added for it were dropped in the round 1 review. Tests only.

## Mechanism (file:line at 5b8df8f5c)

Two writers of client["pingAt"], each atomic under the client's qlock, race in ORDER:

- The clear. `_note_ws_inbound` (kernel/kernel.py:53837; the write at 53843 under the qlock) sets pingAt to None: the peer spoke, no ping is outstanding. In the kernel it runs on the handler thread, on a pong or a message (77668, 77671) and in the `finally` of a dispatch (77688 to 77689), after which the loop top sets inRead = True (77665). In the test the main thread calls it at line 211, standing in for the return from a dispatch.
- The stamp. `_ws_sender` (53700), the client's ws-send thread, when a ping frame reaches the socket (53715 to 53718): under the qlock, if pingAt is None, pingAt = _ws_clock(). The liveness clock starts when the ping reaches the socket, not when the beat queued it. The test's clock at that beat is 1800000090.0.

Line 209 (`_keepalive_all(now=t0 + 3 * WS_DEAD_S)`) queues the second beat's ping; line 211 clears; line 212 asserts None. Line 207 waited for the first ping to leave; nothing waited for the second. The stamp before 211 is a no-op (pingAt was t0, not None); after 212 the test passes; between 211 and 212 it reds with CI's exact value. Two facts at two moments: "no ping is outstanding" (the clear, at 211) and "the second ping has left" (the stamp, whenever the sender reaches the socket). The kernel is consistent under both orders: a ping that leaves after the clear is a new outstanding ping, stamped at its leave time and judged on a window from there, so production never drops a live peer from this. Only the test asserted one order.

The window in microseconds, traced unforced on the unfixed test (every traced run green): in the diagnosis (30 runs, 3.14t) the stamp landed 68 to 158 us after the clear while the main thread was past line 212 within 23 to 51 us of it; re-traced for this change, 10 runs per interpreter, the stamp landed 127.2 to 170.9 us (3.12) and 80.3 to 196.1 us (3.14t) after the clear while the main thread was past the assertion within 1.8 to 3.5 us (3.12) and 3.2 to 4.5 us (3.14t). The re-trace's marker is the entry of the third `_keepalive_all` call, the first statement after the assertion; the diagnosis does not state its marker, the likely reason the second figures differ; both traces put the stamp roughly 100 us behind the main thread. The red needs one preemption of the main thread in that gap. The GIL hid it in every unforced run here: after the enqueue at 209 the main thread runs bytecode to 212 with no blocking call, and the woken sender gets the GIL only on a switch-interval drop request or a blocking call by the holder; no unforced run on 3.12 had one land in the gap (10 of 10 green), and the free-threaded build needs neither. The interleaving is legal under the GIL all the same, and the same forcing reds 3.12. A two-vCPU hosted runner with four competing threads (main, sender, handler, peer) produced it once; a 60-core box did not in 320 free-threaded runs, nor in the 10 on 3.12.

## The guard on the wait (review round 1, 2026-09-21)

## The late order, run on purpose, and why the kernel is unchanged

test_c3c (343) runs the late order through the same instrument and asserts `forcing.forced` (365), so a pass never comes from the scheduler happening to order the writes: the late ping is stamped at its leave time (pingAt == 1800000090.0 right after the clear, 366) and the peer is judged on a window from there (alive at WS_DEAD_S - 1 later, dropped at WS_DEAD_S). Mutation proof, since the semantics it pins hold at the base: the kernel one-hold the reviewer ruled out, emulated in a scratch copy of kernel/kernel.py (`_note_ws_inbound` sets a flag on the client under its qlock after the clear; `_ws_sender` skips one ping stamp while the flag is set): at the build, test_c3c red 3 of 3 on 3.12 and 3 of 3 on 3.14t, `AssertionError: None != 1800000090.0 : the late ping is a new outstanding ping, stamped as it left`; at the round 2 tree, once per interpreter over the module, 3 failed and 11 passed: test_c3c at that assertion, and test_c3 and test_c3f at the shared body's wait for the third ping's stamp (324, `False is not true`), because the fixed body clears only after the second ping's stamp has landed, so the stamp the flag makes the sender skip is the third beat's and pingAt stays at the None the clear left until the settle expires; the other 11 green, test_c3h among them, since it makes no clear. Suppressing the late stamp would leave a ping on the wire never judged, so the obvious kernel fix introduces a real defect where the test fix introduces none. kernel/kernel.py is untouched; the one-hold appears only as an emulated mutation in a scratch copy, the proof that test_c3c would catch it.

## The judge's read order, already pinned

`_keepalive_all` (54239) reads inRead (54254) before pingAt (54258). The comment at 54254 to 54257 says why: the handler ends a dispatch by clearing pingAt (77689) and THEN setting inRead (77665), so a beat between those two writes, read the other way round, pairs a stale ping with a fresh read state and drops a live peer (a 2026-09-04 review find). That order has been pinned since 2026-09-04 by tests/test_view_deltas.py, TwoThreadsOneClient.test_c_the_beat_reads_the_read_state_before_the_ping_stamp, in the fork and in the project: its modelled dict hands the pre-end state to the first of the two reads and the post state to the second, so a swapped read order pairs a stale stamp with a fresh read state and reds there. The round 1 review's refutation executed the swap (`pa = c.get("pingAt")` moved above `in_read = c.get("inRead", True)` in `_keepalive_all`, in scratch copies) at 5b8df8f5c and at the project's main: 2 failed, that test and the first cut's test_c3d, against a clean baseline over the two modules; the first cut's own census of that swap, 13 others green, was scoped to tests/test_ws_liveness.py. This change therefore adds no pin on the read order: the two cases its first cut added for it (test_c3d, a handler returning from a dispatch between the judge's two reads, and test_c3e, a handler entering one) and their instrument were dropped in the round, since the property is already guarded and a duplicate guard only costs upkeep. The diagnosis's measurement stands as history: it forced the pair both ways, the shipped order dropping no live peer 3 of 3 under 3.14t and 3.12, a control with the reads swapped dropping a live peer 3 of 3 under both.

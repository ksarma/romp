Upstream's copy of tests/test_ws_liveness.py carries the same harness race: test_c3 clears pingAt (its stand-in for the handler's return from a dispatch) and asserts None at once, while the second beat's ping, queued two lines earlier, may still be on its way to the socket, where the sender stamps pingAt with the clock. Both orders are valid kernel states, so the kernel is unchanged: the test now waits for that ping to reach the peer before it clears, the wait is guarded by a case that runs the same body under the forced late order and reds when the wait is deleted, the late order is run on purpose by its own case, and the forcing instrument's release of a parked sender on exit is exercised by a committed case at a bound that can show it. The judge's read order (inRead before pingAt in _keepalive_all) was already pinned before this change by tests/test_view_deltas.py, TwoThreadsOneClient.test_c_the_beat_reads_the_read_state_before_the_ping_stamp (in the fork since 2026-09-04 and in the project), which reds on the swap, so this change adds no pin on it; the two cases the first cut added for it were dropped in the round 1 review. Tests only.

## Origin

Found by the CPython 3.14t (free-threaded) CI cell on fork PR 864 (heldmail-readers), whose diff touched nothing on the path: every function on the path (_ws_sender, _note_ws_inbound, _keepalive_all, _new_ws_client, _ws_send, _client_send, _dist_ver, the handler loop) and the test module are byte-identical between that PR's merge-base and its head, and the test module is byte-identical again at 5b8df8f5c, this change's base. One red in that cell (`AssertionError: 1800000090.0 is not None` at tests/test_ws_liveness.py:212; the other 17227 tests passed); unforced, 320 of 320 green on 3.14t on the 60-core box that diagnosed it (10 and then 100 runs at each of the two trees, 100 more at the merge-base pinned to two cores) and 10 of 10 on 3.12. Proved latent at the merge-base by forcing the order: under the late forcing the unfixed test reds at that PR's merge-base (3.14t and 3.12), at its head (3.14t) and at 5b8df8f5c (both interpreters).

## Mechanism (file:line at 5b8df8f5c)

Two writers of client["pingAt"], each atomic under the client's qlock, race in ORDER:

- The clear. `_note_ws_inbound` (kernel/kernel.py:53837; the write at 53843 under the qlock) sets pingAt to None: the peer spoke, no ping is outstanding. In the kernel it runs on the handler thread, on a pong or a message (77668, 77671) and in the `finally` of a dispatch (77688 to 77689), after which the loop top sets inRead = True (77665). In the test the main thread calls it at line 211, standing in for the return from a dispatch.
- The stamp. `_ws_sender` (53700), the client's ws-send thread, when a ping frame reaches the socket (53715 to 53718): under the qlock, if pingAt is None, pingAt = _ws_clock(). The liveness clock starts when the ping reaches the socket, not when the beat queued it. The test's clock at that beat is 1800000090.0.

Line 209 (`_keepalive_all(now=t0 + 3 * WS_DEAD_S)`) queues the second beat's ping; line 211 clears; line 212 asserts None. Line 207 waited for the first ping to leave; nothing waited for the second. The stamp before 211 is a no-op (pingAt was t0, not None); after 212 the test passes; between 211 and 212 it reds with CI's exact value. Two facts at two moments: "no ping is outstanding" (the clear, at 211) and "the second ping has left" (the stamp, whenever the sender reaches the socket). The kernel is consistent under both orders: a ping that leaves after the clear is a new outstanding ping, stamped at its leave time and judged on a window from there, so production never drops a live peer from this. Only the test asserted one order.

The window in microseconds, traced unforced on the unfixed test (every traced run green): in the diagnosis (30 runs, 3.14t) the stamp landed 68 to 158 us after the clear while the main thread was past line 212 within 23 to 51 us of it; re-traced for this change, 10 runs per interpreter, the stamp landed 127.2 to 170.9 us (3.12) and 80.3 to 196.1 us (3.14t) after the clear while the main thread was past the assertion within 1.8 to 3.5 us (3.12) and 3.2 to 4.5 us (3.14t). The re-trace's marker is the entry of the third `_keepalive_all` call, the first statement after the assertion; the diagnosis does not state its marker, the likely reason the second figures differ; both traces put the stamp roughly 100 us behind the main thread. The red needs one preemption of the main thread in that gap. The GIL hid it in every unforced run here: after the enqueue at 209 the main thread runs bytecode to 212 with no blocking call, and the woken sender gets the GIL only on a switch-interval drop request or a blocking call by the holder; no unforced run on 3.12 had one land in the gap (10 of 10 green), and the free-threaded build needs neither. The interleaving is legal under the GIL all the same, and the same forcing reds 3.12. A two-vCPU hosted runner with four competing threads (main, sender, handler, peer) produced it once; a 60-core box did not in 320 free-threaded runs, nor in the 10 on 3.12.

## The fix, and the forcing in both directions (the reviewer's condition one)

test_c3 now waits for the second beat's ping to reach the peer, `self.assertTrue(self._settle(lambda: len(peer.pings) == 2), ...)` (line 359, in `_a_dispatch_return_gets_a_fresh_window`, 346 to 366, the body test_c3 has called since the round 1 review), before it clears (360) and asserts None (361), the way test_b already waited; every other assertion is unchanged. The harness is keyed on the event it assumed (the ping left) instead of on the scheduler. Module line numbers from here on are at the tree of the round 1 follow-up's commit.

The forcing is `_StampClearForcing` (tests/test_ws_liveness.py:93 to 203), a test-local instrument over the two module functions the harness threads look up by name, so the real sender runs: a queue proxy holds the second ping before its stamp, a socket-lock proxy marks the stamp point (the sender takes that lock right after the stamp), and the wrapped `_note_ws_inbound` is the clear. `late` holds the stamp until the clear has landed and lets the clear return only once the stamp point has passed; `early` is the converse, the clear waits for the stamp point first. Every wait is bounded (1.0 s) and `holds` records whether the event or the bound released it; `forced` is True only when every hold was released by its event. Since the round 1 review, leaving the block releases both holds whatever the path (`__exit__`, 199 to 203, sets the two events before it restores the functions), so a test that raises inside the block strands no sender thread in a hold, and a wait released that way is recorded as not by the event; `_connect` (243 to 258) records the factory's ws-send thread from the thread enumeration around the factory call, so tearDown's outlived-thread assertion (232) covers it. Since the round's follow-up the instrument also carries two events a test can wait on, `parked` (set as a hold begins its wait) and `released` (set as a hold's wait returns, after its record is complete), and two more trace stamps, `at["exit"]` in `__exit__` before the sets and `at["hold_returned"]` as a hold returns unreleased by its event; a hold released by its event leaves `at` with the two write stamps alone, which test_c3g's keyset assertion reads. The executed proof of the release and the recording is in the guard section below. Runs: five pytest processes per cell, the forcing applied by a plugin that takes the instrument from the committed module's source, env stripped of every ROMP_* variable and CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID, `-p no:cacheprovider`, XDG_STATE_HOME under a scratch with session-hosts off, `sys._is_gil_enabled()` recorded from inside pytest (False on every 3.14t run; 3.14.6 free-threading here, 3.14.7 in CI), CPython 3.12.3 the other interpreter.

- Unfixed test_c3, from a git archive of 5b8df8f5c. Late: 0 of 5 pass on 3.12 and 0 of 5 on 3.14t, every run `AssertionError: 1800000090.0 is not None` at line 212, CI's text, the forcing satisfied (both holds released by their events). Early: 5 of 5 pass on each interpreter.
- Fixed test_c3. Early: 5 of 5 pass on 3.12 and 5 of 5 on 3.14t, the forcing satisfied. Late: 5 of 5 pass on 3.12 and 5 of 5 on 3.14t; in all 10 runs the hold on the stamp expired at its bound unreleased, because the fixed test clears only after the second ping has reached the peer, which is after its stamp: the late order is unreachable from the fixed test, not tolerated by it. The failure mode the condition guards against, a fix that swaps which order the test asserts, would red under the early forcing; this fix passes both. Since the round 1 review the guarded late case, test_c3f, asserts that hold expiry from the instrument's record (the guard section below).
- Unforced, the whole module at the build: 14 of 14 in five runs per interpreter at the fixed tree, and 14 of 14 on 3.14t at the committed head. After the round 1 review (two cases dropped, two added): 14 of 14 in 5 of 5 runs per interpreter (CPython 3.12.3 and CPython 3.14.6 free-threading, the GIL flag read False in-process on every 3.14t run) at the round's tree in the worktree, and 14 of 14 once per interpreter at the round's committed tests head. ## The guard on the wait (review round 1, 2026-09-21)

The round found the wait unguarded: deleting it left all 14 cases in the module green, so the flake this change removes could return with every test passing. A new case carrying its own copy of test_c3's body does not catch the deletion either, since the copy is untouched by it (the round's refutation executed that); so test_c3's body is now the method `_a_dispatch_return_gets_a_fresh_window` (346 to 366; the wait at 359, the clear at 360, the assertion at 361), called by test_c3 (323) and by two cases that run it under `_StampClearForcing` after `arm()`. What the wait buys: the fixed body clears only after the second ping has reached the peer, which is after its stamp point, so the late order is unreachable from it.

test_c3f (413) is the guard: the shared body under `late`. The hold on the stamp cannot be released by the clear, so it expires at its bound (1.0 s, inside `_settle`'s 3.0 s) and the clear's return finds the stamp point already passed. The case asserts that from the instrument's record, hold by hold (440 to 443): the stamp hold released by its bound, the clear's return hold by its event. Two kinds of assertion, and the docstrings of test_c3 and test_c3f say which is which since the follow-up: the helper's `assertIsNone` (361) is the product assertion, establishing that the outcome was right (no ping outstanding after the clear); test_c3f's two `assertIn` over `holds` are the instrument's, establishing that the forcing happened as the fixed body dictates; test_c3 is the ordinary-path case, the body unforced, and its docstring names test_c3f as the guard of the wait line. Mutation proof, in a scratch copy of the reviewed tree with the round's module copied in and the wait deleted from the shared body (kernel byte-identical): test_c3f red 3 of 3 on CPython 3.12.3 and 3 of 3 on CPython 3.14.6 free-threading (the GIL flag read False in-process), every run at the shared body's `assertIsNone(client["pingAt"])` with `AssertionError: 1800000090.0 is not None`, CI's text; test_c3 green 3 of 3 per interpreter through the same body (unforced, the stamp lands about a hundred microseconds after the main thread has passed the assertion, the reason the guard is a separate case over a shared body); the whole module once per interpreter, 1 failed (test_c3f) and 13 passed, test_c3g among the green by design. Re-run in the follow-up's scratch copy (an archive of the round's head with the follow-up's module copied in and the wait deleted, kernel byte-identical), once per interpreter: 1 failed (test_c3f, at the shared body's `assertIsNone` with CI's text) and 14 passed, test_c3 and test_c3g green, test_c3h green. As shipped, at the round's tree in the worktree: the module 14 passed in 5 of 5 runs per interpreter; test_c3f green 3 of 3 alone per interpreter (about 1.2 s each, the 1.0 s hold expiry); once more per interpreter at the round's committed tests head, 14 passed.

The early order. test_c3g (445) is the committed selection of `early`: the shared body under it, asserting `forced` (459), that both moments were traced (460) and that the stamp point preceded the clear (`at["stamp"] < at["clear"]`, 461); deterministic and fast (0.22 to 0.26 s: the clear's hold finds the stamp point already passed), green 3 of 3 alone per interpreter and inside every module run above. So the both-directions evidence of the section above is reproducible from the tree: the early order by test_c3g (the forcing satisfied, the stamp point before the clear), the late order by test_c3f (its stamp hold expires by design) and by test_c3c (run on purpose, the forcing satisfied). test_c3g does not guard the wait and is not claimed to: with the wait deleted the clear's hold waits for the stamp point, which the unfixed order still reaches, so it stayed green in the mutation copy (once per interpreter in the round and once more per interpreter in the follow-up's re-run, inside the module runs that red test_c3f alone).

The instrument's release on exit, executed in the round once per interpreter per cell in three scratch copies of the same archive with test_c3f's forcing given hold_s=10.0 (the shared body's wait for the second ping then fails at its 3.0 s bound while the sender is parked in the stamp hold): as shipped, 1 failed at that wait (`AssertionError: False is not true : the second ping has left: no stamp of it is pending`) and no error, tearDown green; with the two set() calls removed from `__exit__`, 1 failed and 1 error at teardown, `AssertionError: True is not false : a test thread outlived its test: <Thread(ws-send, started daemon ...)>`; with the recording in `_connect` removed as well, 1 failed and no error, tearDown blind again. That proof lived only in a scratch, because the shipped 1.0 s hold expires before tearDown's 3.0 s join, so no committed case could show a parked sender at the shipped bound. Since the follow-up the release is exercised by the committed case test_c3h (463) at a 10 s hold: the late forcing parks the real sender before the second ping's stamp (the case waits for `parked`, 501, so the release is of a thread that is parked, not of one that finds the event already set), the test raises a test-local exception before any clear (503), and after the block the case asserts on the event with a generous bound behind it rather than on a wait that cannot tell its release from its expiry: `released` within 30 s (504), whose red says the sender was never released; the hold's return within half the hold of `at["exit"]` (507), whose red says the bound expired and the exit released nothing (half, not the whole hold, because the hold begins before the exit and the bound's expiry can land on either side of hold_s after it; the exit's release lands within milliseconds); `exited` True and the stamp hold recorded as not released by its event (508, 509); then, as the consequence and only because the release is already proven, the peer's second ping (513). Mutation and counts: in a scratch copy over an archive of the round's head with the follow-up's module copied in (kernel byte-identical) and the two set() calls removed from `__exit__`, test_c3h red once on CPython 3.12.3 and once on CPython 3.14.6 free-threading at the elapsed assertion, `released AFTER the bound expired, not by the exit`, the hold returned 10.000052 s and 10.000175 s after the exit (52 and 175 microseconds past the whole hold, the reason the threshold is half of it), the other 14 cases green and no error at teardown (by the time tearDown joins, the expired hold has freed the sender; a parked sender shows at the join only when a case leaves the block with hold time left, which this case does not, as it waits for the return first). As shipped, test_c3h green 3 of 3 alone per interpreter, 0.02 to 0.04 s of call time, and inside the module's 5 of 5 per interpreter; in one traced run per interpreter the hold returned 92 microseconds (3.12) and 23 microseconds (3.14t) after the exit.

## The late order, run on purpose

test_c3c (line 382) runs the late order through the same instrument and asserts `forcing.forced`, so a pass never comes from the scheduler happening to order the writes: the late ping is stamped at its leave time (pingAt == 1800000090.0 right after the clear) and the peer is judged on a window from there (alive at WS_DEAD_S - 1 later, dropped at WS_DEAD_S). Mutation proof, since the semantics it pins hold at the base: the kernel one-hold the reviewer ruled out, emulated in a scratch copy of kernel/kernel.py (`_note_ws_inbound` sets a flag on the client under its qlock after the clear; `_ws_sender` skips one ping stamp while the flag is set): test_c3c red 3 of 3 on 3.12 and 3 of 3 on 3.14t, `AssertionError: None != 1800000090.0 : the late ping is a new outstanding ping, stamped as it left`; fixed test_c3 red too, at its wait for the third ping's stamp (line 363), since its third beat uses the same clock value as the clear; the other 12 tests green.

## The judge's read order, already pinned

`_keepalive_all` (54239) reads inRead (54254) before pingAt (54258). The comment at 54254 to 54257 says why: the handler ends a dispatch by clearing pingAt (77689) and THEN setting inRead (77665), so a beat between those two writes, read the other way round, pairs a stale ping with a fresh read state and drops a live peer (a 2026-09-04 review find). That order has been pinned since 2026-09-04 by tests/test_view_deltas.py, TwoThreadsOneClient.test_c_the_beat_reads_the_read_state_before_the_ping_stamp, in the fork and in the project: its modelled dict hands the pre-end state to the first of the two reads and the post state to the second, so a swapped read order pairs a stale stamp with a fresh read state and reds there. The round 1 review's refutation executed the swap (`pa = c.get("pingAt")` moved above `in_read = c.get("inRead", True)` in `_keepalive_all`, in scratch copies) at 5b8df8f5c and at the project's main: 2 failed, that test and the first cut's test_c3d, against a clean baseline over the two modules; the first cut's own census of that swap, 13 others green, was scoped to tests/test_ws_liveness.py. This change therefore adds no pin on the read order: the two cases its first cut added for it (test_c3d, a handler returning from a dispatch between the judge's two reads, and test_c3e, a handler entering one) and their instrument were dropped in the round, since the property is already guarded and a duplicate guard only costs upkeep. The diagnosis's measurement stands as history: it forced the pair both ways, the shipped order dropping no live peer 3 of 3 under 3.14t and 3.12, a control with the reads swapped dropping a live peer 3 of 3 under both.

## Why the kernel is unchanged

Suppressing the late stamp would leave a ping on the wire never judged, so the obvious kernel fix introduces a real defect where the test fix introduces none. kernel/kernel.py is untouched; the one-hold appears only as an emulated mutation in a scratch copy, the proof that test_c3c would catch it.

## Also in the change

The module mints its own state root at import and now writes `off` into `<root>/romp/session-hosts` (line 31), the suite's rule for a test that mints a state root; the module never connects a session, so the line changes no behaviour. The state-isolation order scan (tests/test_state_isolation_order.py) passes over the changed module.

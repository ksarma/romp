`_PerfStats.snapshot()` added judge.py's module-global pool counter (`jd.judge_worker_cpu_ms()`, fed by `_TimedPool`) to its COPY of `judge.cpu_ms_sum` at every read while the live dict never carried it, so a live read and a snapshot read of one counter disagreed by the whole total and a delta taken from one of each was wrong (the child-road test paid, then read both from the snapshot). The workers' CPU now lands in the live dict as each pool future ends: judge.py exposes `set_worker_cpu_sink(fn)` (a callable it holds, in the direction the module already takes for `set_sdk_owner_provider` and `run_pass(before_tier=...)`), the kernel arms it with `_PerfStats.judge_worker_cpu` right after building the collector, and the snapshot copies `cpu_ms_sum` and `cpu_ms_workers` without adding. The module counter and `judge_worker_cpu_ms()` stay for the serve child, which runs judge.py with no kernel in its process and reports `workerCpuMs` from it; the child road's fold in `judge_child_done` is unchanged. Upstream carries the read-time fold (upstream/main db73909e6, kernel/kernel.py `snapshot()` `judge["cpu_ms_sum"] += workers`). Tests: a live read and a snapshot agree after pool work (red before), the child's report lands once with `cpu_ms_workers` flat, the sink is optional (a judge module no kernel loaded keeps its counter), and a child process shows the kernel arms the sink at load. The review ruling (2026-09-18) took the fold with one condition: an unarmed sink must be distinguishable from a genuine zero, since with the read-time fold gone a process that never armed the sink would report 0.0 worker time as fact. So the live dict starts WITHOUT `cpu_ms_workers` and `_PerfStats.arm_judge_worker_sink` (the kernel's one call after building the collector; a test's setUp) opens the key at 0.0 and installs the sink: a judge block without the key is from a collector nothing armed, no new key is added to the snapshot's vocabulary, `romp perf` prints "workers' share not reported" beside the judge share and cpu/pass over such a pair, and `set_worker_cpu_sink`'s docstring names the unarmed reading and who arms it, as `set_sdk_owner_provider`'s does.

2026-09-19: review round 1 tightened the presence rule and pinned it both ways. The key `cpu_ms_workers` rides a served judge block exactly while THAT collector is the sink judge.py holds (`_PerfStats.holds_judge_worker_sink`, asking judge.py's new `worker_cpu_sink()` at snapshot time and at reset): a collector a later kernel load displaced (the load re-executes judge.py and clears the hook; a test-process shape) had kept the key frozen at 0.0 and served it as a measurement, and `reset()` had dropped the key from an armed collector; both fixed as one invariant, with tests in one process and across two kernel loads in a child process. `_TimedPool`'s finally now restores the worker's stage mark BEFORE the accounting, so a raising sink cannot leave the submitter's mark on the pool thread, and nothing catches the raise. An AST enumeration found two kernel call sites into `jd.run_*`: `run_pass` from `_producer` and `run_rewound_reconcile` from `_rewind_migration_bg` on a boot thread; the reconcile reaches no pool site (call-graph walk over judge.py) and a test runs the real migration with a recording sink and shows zero futures; a plain-thread pool future is shown to land under the same keys. The read-time fold's own entry (`2026-09-18-judges-perf-baseline.md`) carries a dated line naming this change.

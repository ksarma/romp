Upstream's pane hold (#1104, #1123) has one exit, the pane's own ending event, so a wedged upload that never acks or nacks pins the page on an old build indefinitely; the fork adds a 60 s backstop for the upload and held-send words. The clock is the pane word's: it starts when the word first blocks an owed reload and resets when the word ends or changes (a hold released and re-raised gets its own 60 s); at 60 s the reload fires anyway, one console line names the word and the seconds, and the fresh page's replayed toasts carry a note saying which hold had not finished. The five gesture words (pointer, pan, drag, selection, typing) are never clocked; busy() reads each window's gesture (busyHere) and its pane word (paneHere, the window's __rompPaneBusy alone) apart, so a gesture in any window, the word's own pane included, leaves the clock alone; busy() reports a gesture anywhere ahead of every pane word, and a gesture up at the deadline defers the release to its own ending event, so a reload still never lands mid-gesture. The shim's 'sends' is a pane word with no deadline either (the NOCLOCK set: no timer, no console line, no note). It is reported ahead of a clocked word and defers a release past the deadline like a gesture, so an upload beside it clocks from its first sight and is released only on the flush. That word is true only while the pane's socket is closed with prompts queued for the reopen, so a reload over it would land on a kernel not answering the page and take the queued prompt with it; the flush in ws.onopen is its only end, and the shim redials within 1.5 s, so a live kernel ends the hold within seconds. A refused reload (a host that forbids location.reload) drops the note and the clock and re-persists the toasts without it. Landed in the 2026-09-09 fold (slice 1) on the reviewer's ruling; upstream at bcc7e215 has none of it.

The guard on `_revive_postal_bus` adopted at the fold from the project's tip (fork main had no such guard, and `_BUS_ENSURED` with its four lines is the project's text too) read `ROMP_POSTAL_CLIENT_ONLY` alone, one term of the postal service's own authority (`is_client_only()` is peers OFF and, the variable or the marker file), and so, by the refuters' execution: it fired for a client-only host designated by the environment word read as true, missed the road where client-only is designated by the marker file alone (a revive there costs a ping and no spawn), and separately, with peers on, the default, skipped the revive for a kernel that did own a bus; beside it `_BUS_ENSURED` was armed on any exit-0 `ensure`, the client-only ping of a tunnel included, so `_bus_port`'s stated precondition, a bus of this kernel's own, was not what the flag measured. The corrected shape keeps the environment's word (read as "this kernel must start no bus", stricter than the service's mode on purpose: mirroring `is_client_only()` would let the suite's floor, the word with peers on, spawn a real bus off a refused notify on a daemon thread) and keys the skip on the kernel's own ensured flag, `if no_bus_word and not _BUS_ENSURED[0]: return`, which the ensure runner arms only on the owned roads that `ensure` now reports: `ensure_road()` returns (ok, road) with the roads spawned, up, answering, client-only, refused and down (`up` requires the bus's own port record to name a live pid under this token's mark, so a stale tunnel or another environment's bus answering the port is `answering`, owned by nobody here), the CLI verb prints `ensure: road=<road>` on stdout, `ensure()` keeps its bool contract for the in-process callers, and the kernel reads the line (`_ensure_road`) and arms `_BUS_ENSURED` for `spawned` and `up` alone (postal_service.ENSURE_OWNED, mirrored as the kernel's `_BUS_OWNED_ROADS`), says an unowned road once on stderr (once per road, a change said again: `_BUS_ROAD_SAID`, the sibling of the port census's memory, since `_revive_postal_bus` re-runs the ensure on every refused notify; review round 2), and once armed leaves the flag standing. `docs/reference.md`'s `ROMP_POSTAL_PORT` paragraph is restated from `_bus_port`'s docstring, the one authoritative enumeration, and goes with this offer: the variable is the fallback for any record the kernel cannot trust as its own bus's (absent or unreadable, stale with its pid no longer running, or another bus's with a token mark that is not this kernel's), or when its ensure at boot neither started the bus nor found the machine's own answering. The refuters' evidence, by execution: the re-cut pin in tests/test_kernel_trust.py (exit 1, and exit 0 on the client-only, answering or unnamed road, arm nothing; spawned and up arm; armed once, the flag stands) and the new tests/test_postal_bus_revive_guard.py (a private state root with session-hosts off, the ensure a stub or its ping stubbed, Popen a recorder, never a real bus: the floor's world skips, a kernel that ensured a bus of its own revives word or no word, a client-only host never revives, the six roads and the verb's stdout line end to end) are red before the fix, 13 cases across the two modules against the fold's merged text, and green after (52), with the served floor's bus census unchanged before and after the run, and review round 2's said-once case is red without the latch (2 failed of 17 in the guard module, the latch absent by name; 1 failed with the declaration alone) and green with it; the fix is a fork divergence of adopted text and an upstream candidate.

Every served lab drew its kernels' ports by binding port 0, reading the port and closing the socket, and waited for a kernel by polling /healthz until anything answered. Two such draws in a row can return the same port (16 equal pairs in 200,000 back-to-back draws, measured on Linux), and nothing checked. tests/test_federated_cutfloor_served.py drew its remote's and its hub's port back to back; when the two were equal the hub could not bind, its wait read the remote's answer, and the check-in to the hub got the remote's 403, an error at setup naming neither cause (the fork's CI, 2026-10-01; forced, it reds that module and tests/test_timeline_same_name_served.py on every run). The same draws could give a one-kernel lab a postal port equal to its serve port. tests/lab_ports.py is now the one door: reserve(lab) hands out a port no live reservation of the process holds and, on Linux, keeps its socket bound so no other bind to port 0 on the machine is given it until release(lab), which each lab calls after its kernels are reaped; kernel_env reserves the postal port under the kernel's lab; wait_owned(proc, env) returns None only once /healthz answers with the kernel's own pid in X-Romp-Boot and the kernel's state root records the port in serve-port, and otherwise returns the reason, naming whatever answered, for the lab's skip. All 100 of the fork's served modules that boot a kernel moved to it. tests/test_lab_ports_census.py derives the population from the served step and holds it to the door (no draw and release, no hand-written /healthz wait, a wait_owned in every function that starts the kernel, a release in every module that reserves and on the failure path of every class that reserves in setUpClass, the door imported by its own name), and tests/test_lab_ports.py executes the door against stand-ins and against two real kernels handed one port. Tests only, so docs tier.

The reservation's hold is Linux behaviour: off Linux the helper keeps only its process table and the ownership proof, and CI runs the served labs on Linux.

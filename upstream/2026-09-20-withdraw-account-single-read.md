Found by the adversarial verification of round five of https://github.com/romp-on/romp/pull/994 at head a3d4d7169 (2026-09-20) and scoped out of that round, because the fix changes what the surfaces say, a contract plans/user-todos.md states for the withdraw account, and no row is lost or misstamped through any window. The class: a surface that makes one decision over the request store (an account, a press's outcome, a card build, a signature value, a verdict line) from more than one read of the store, taking its branch on one read's verdict and wording its answer from another read's contents. The readers take no lock and stat the file on every call (_user_todos_read: a cache keyed on mtime_ns and size; a stat or read raising an OSError other than FileNotFoundError answers the empty stand-in beside a fault flag), so a fault that begins or lifts between two of the reads gives the one decision two verdicts on one file, and the answer is worded from the wrong one. The withdraw account is the first instance, its three windows executed by the project's reviewer in round five (https://github.com/romp-on/romp/pull/994#issuecomment-5752139961); the reviewer named three more surfaces of the same shape, and the derivation below turns up a fifth. Not data loss anywhere: every write is refused or complete on the writer's own read and the row on disk is right in every window; only what one call, press, build or verdict line says is wrong.

Every line number in this entry is read at head a3d4d7169 of https://github.com/romp-on/romp/pull/994 and moves with it; the head sha is the dated anchor of the observation.

A further instance is recognisable by the property alone: a path whose branch rests on one read of the store and whose answer is worded from another is in the class, whatever it decides; a path whose answer rests on the read it words from is not, however many times it read (the register route, below). The store lock is not part of the property. It serializes writers, the readers never take it, and of the five surfaces only the withdraw account holds it, so a census keyed on the lock finds one surface, not five.

The withdraw account, _withdraw_user_todo, holds the store lock for its whole section and reads the store three times inside it: _user_todos_unreadable() at 8860, the row lookup through _user_todos() at 8863 (the _row helper at 8856), and a re-read through the same helper at 8869 after the stamp at 8868 (_resolve_user_todo, whose own read at 8822 is a fourth). Three windows, each executed by the reviewer against a3d4d7169 and confirmed as written below.

First window: a fault beginning after _user_todos_unreadable() answered healthy at 8860 and before the row lookup at 8863. The lookup reads the empty stand-in, finds no row, and the account is state unknown with owner False, the "not yours" wording the check was added to keep off an unreadable store.

Second window: a fault beginning after the lookup at 8863 found the row open and before the stamp at 8868. _resolve_user_todo reads the stand-in, finds no open row and writes nothing (ok False); the re-read at 8869 finds no row either, so the pre-stamp row stands in, its resolved field empty, and the account is the "malformed closing stamp on the id: resolved=None" error with ok False and owner True, on a healthy open row.

Third window: a fault beginning at the re-read at 8869 after a stamp that landed at 8868. The same malformed-stamp error, with ok True, while the row on disk carries a well-formed withdrawn stamp.

The Reply (userTodoAnswer), the first of the two drive ops the reviewer named, checks _user_todos_unreadable() at 21306 and looks the row up through _open_user_todos() at 21309. A fault beginning between them words the press "That request was already settled: nothing was sent" on an open row. The op has a third read, found in the code at the same head while filing and not executed: after the handover at 21316, the stamp at 21321 (_stamp_user_todo_answered, through _resolve_user_todo's own read at 8822) answers False on a flagged read, having found no open row on the stand-in, and the op discards the False. A fault beginning between the lookup and the stamp therefore leaves the answer delivered, the row open and nothing said to the person. The arm at 21322 that says the answer could not be recorded is reached by a stamp that raised, and a flagged read returns before the write's refusal.

The Dismiss (userTodoDismiss), the second drive op, has the same shape; its lines are read at the same head while filing. It checks at 21338 and stamps through _resolve_user_todo at 21342, and a fault beginning between them answers False the same way, so the press is worded "That request was already settled: the session withdrew it, or it was answered moments ago" on an open row. Its arm for a store that went bad under the check (21344) is reached by a RuntimeError, which a flagged read never raises there.

The chat signature, _user_todo_fp, named by the reviewer, reads this sid's rows through _user_todos() at 9289 and, finding none, asks _user_todos_unreadable() at 9291. A fault lifting between them answers None, the value of a sid with no rows, for a sid whose rows the store holds; a fault beginning between them answers "unreadable" off a read that was healthy. Either way one build's signature is worded from two reads.

The card in build_session, named by the reviewer, reads the open rows through _open_user_todos() at 41125 and, when there are none, asks _user_todos_unreadable() at 41142. A fault lifting between them answers no rows and no error line: the session's open requests leave the card for that build with no word, the silent fold the branch at 41142 was added against. A fault beginning between them, for a session with no open rows, puts the unreadable line on a card whose rows were read healthy.

The loss seam, _user_todo_answer_lost, is the fifth surface: the derivation below turns it up, the reviewer's roster does not name it, and it is read from the code at the same head, not executed. Under the store lock it calls _reopen_user_todo at 9434 (its own read at 9011) and, when that answers False, reads the store again at 9451 to word its verdict line open or stale. A flagged read inside _reopen_user_todo answers False before the write's refusal, so a fault at that read never reaches the seam's arm for a refused reopen (9435, the line saying the user still owes the session an answer): the row stays answered, and the verdict line, worded from the second read, says no answered row exists to reopen (cleared meanwhile, or evicted by the cap). A fault beginning between the two reads, for a row the first read found open, words the line stale where open was true. The boot pass hands a persisted drop mark over again, so the reopen is late, not lost.

How to re-derive the population at a head, first the roster of readers. Every read of the store goes through _user_todos_read: `git show <head>:kernel/kernel.py | grep -n 'user-todos.json'` shows the file opened for reading at one site (8598 at this head; 8695 is the writer). Its callers are the two wrapper readers (_user_todos, _user_todos_unreadable), the five readers built on them (_open_user_todos, _blocking_user_todos, _user_todo_context_block, _user_todo_fp, and _row inside the withdraw account) and the five writers that read inside (_add_user_todo, _resolve_user_todo, _reopen_user_todo, _stamp_user_todo_answered, _prune_user_todos). The roster is kept by hand: a new helper that reads the store must be added to the pattern below, and the file-path grep is the check that no read bypasses _user_todos_read.

Then the census. The command: `git show a3d4d7169:kernel/kernel.py | grep -nE '(^|[^A-Za-z0-9_.])(_user_todos|_user_todos_read|_user_todos_unreadable|_open_user_todos|_blocking_user_todos|_user_todo_context_block|_user_todo_fp|_add_user_todo|_resolve_user_todo|_reopen_user_todo|_stamp_user_todo_answered|_prune_user_todos)\(' | grep -vE '^[0-9]+:\s*def '`. At this head it prints 37 lines. Grouped by the function or drive-op branch that contains them, seven paths read the store more than once inside one decision: the withdraw account (8856 through 8863 and 8869, 8860, 8868), the register route (8937, then _add_user_todo at 8942), the signature (9289, 9291), the loss seam (9434, 9451), the Reply (21306, 21309, 21321), the Dismiss (21338, 21342) and the card (41125, 41142); every other site is a path's one read (the prune at 9122 and the boot pass at 9490 say so in their comments). The register route is outside the class: _add_user_todo has no lookup between its read and its write, so a flagged read reaches _write_user_todos and raises, the route catches the RuntimeError at 8945, and its 503 is worded from the write's own verdict. That leaves five surfaces, six paths with the drive ops counted as two. A reader asking whether the count still holds runs both commands at the current head and applies the property to every path with two or more sites.

Remedy, the reviewer's for all five: one read with its verdict at the top of each surface, and the stamp returning its own time. For the withdraw account the section makes one _user_todos_read() call and branches everything on that (store, flag) pair, the unreadable account on the flag and the row lookup on the store; _resolve_user_todo returns the stamp it wrote (the {kind, t} dict, None when it stamped nothing) so the account is worded from the write's own result and no read follows the stamp. _resolve_user_todo's own read is then the one read left inside the section besides the first: either it takes the caller's (store, flag) in place of reading, or a no-stamp return under a flagged read is worded as the unreadable account, which is sound because every writer of the store holds the lock (all four _write_user_todos callers at this head sit under it), so a row the section found open cannot have been closed by another writer before the stamp, and a missing stamp after an open find can only be the store's fault. For the drive ops, the signature and the card, the one read is a _user_todos_read() at the top whose flag words the unreadable branch and whose store feeds the lookup.

Added to the remedy while filing, from the reads above: the stamp and reopen helpers answer a flagged read as False, the same value as no such row, so their callers' arms for a store gone bad under the check (21322, 21344, 37620, 9435) are reached by a failed write or by a flag a reader on another thread set inside the writer's window, and never by a fault on the op's own thread. The helpers should answer the flag distinctly (take the caller's pair, or refuse before the lookup the way _write_user_todos refuses), which closes the Dismiss and the loss seam with the same change. Separately, the Dismiss arm at 21344 catches OSError as Reply's 21322 and the recall's 37620 do, so a failed write is told to the person and the socket kept, since the executed consequence of the miss (the note below) is a torn client connection with nothing said.

Tier fix, with a test that fails before it: a stat fault that begins after a surface's first read (the counted-call shape of the stat fault WriterCarriesItsOwnVerdict in tests/test_user_todos.py uses, aimed at the later stats), asserting for the withdraw account the unreadable account (state unknown, owner None, error naming the store) in the first two windows and, in the third, an account carrying the stamp the call wrote (ok True, state withdrawn, at its time); for the Reply and the Dismiss the unreadable warn, never the settled one; for the signature and the card one value per build, the unreadable one under a fault at either read; for the loss seam the refused-reopen line. The fix builds on _user_todos_read and _user_todos_unreadable, which the fork's main does not carry (both are that PR's).

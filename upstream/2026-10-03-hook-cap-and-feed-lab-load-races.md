Two load-race fixes from fork PR 955 to tests the project also ships: the prompt hook test's change ports verbatim, and the latency lab's needs a hand port. Both are tests only, so docs tier, and whether either is offered to the project is the user's call. The PR's third fix is to a module only the fork carries and is not part of this entry. Every statement below about the project reads its public tree, https://github.com/romp-on/romp at f4a572008.

tests/test_prompt_hook_failopen.py ports verbatim. The cron prompt's hook body makes two thread hops: the registry read, then the cronDelivered write. With the old 0.05 s cap, a loaded machine was still in the read when the cap fired, so the body was cancelled before it called _update_reg, cronDelivered was never written, and the test's last assertion failed. The read now gets 1 s (still a wall-clock budget, twenty times the old one), and the stalled write_reg blocks on an event until the hook has answered. If the hook answers while the write is still held, it did not wait for the write, and no sleep can race that check. On the fork, two reproductions turned the test red without the fix and left it green with it: a forced read delay, and the test pinned to one CPU beside 48 busy loops. Four product mutants (the write on the loop thread, the hook waiting for the cut body, no cap, the slot never recorded) each turn it red. The project's copy of the module is the same blob as the fork's at this PR's base; the code the test exercises (the prompt hook's body through the cronDelivered write, and _update_reg's call to the module-level write_reg) is the same in both trees; and the PR's patch applies to the project's copy unchanged.

The project's copy of tests/test_feed_focus_latency_served.py has the same race, but the fix does not port verbatim. The feed page replaces its cards with every kernel feed frame, and the kernel sends feed frames on its own schedule, one of them a few hundred ms after a settle's deliver. On a loaded machine that frame landed before a road's click found its synthetic card, the card left the page, and the click timed out. The lab's deliver now sets __labBoard, and from then on its wrapIn drops the kernel's feed and feedDelta frames; the roads read the section and the chat and click the lab's own cards. On the fork, with the order forced (the click waits for the kernel's next feed frame), the lab was red 2 of 2 without the fix and green with it; two feed.ts mutants (the feed's own clicks moving nothing locally, a session-name click moving nothing) still turn it red. The project's copy runs the same sequence: its settle makes a real tab change, delivers the lab's board through the same deliver and clicks that board's cards, its wrapIn passes every kernel feed frame on to the shim, and its page replaces its cards with each one. The race has not been measured in the project's tree.

Two differences stop the lab's patch from porting verbatim. The project's kernel has no feedDelta frame: it sends a feed delta as {type:"delta", slot:"feed"}, which the shim reassembles into a whole feed message before the page sees it. And the project's lab lacks fork PR 927's frame hold, the line the patch's wrapIn hunk takes as context. A port:
- sets __labBoard in deliver, as the fork does (the project's deliver is the same as the fork's);
- places the drop by hand in the project's wrapIn;
- drops m.type === "feed", or m.type === "delta" with m.slot === "feed", where the fork drops feed and feedDelta. Dropping whole feed frames alone would leave the race open, since the deltas would still reach the page.

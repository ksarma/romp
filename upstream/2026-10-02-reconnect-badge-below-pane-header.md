The corner badge that says reconnecting over a pane with content (T217) sits at top:8px, right:8px. In the chat, the Outline and the Waiting pane that is over the right end of the header above the pane's list. In a hermetic lab on the fork, on a phone it covered 78 percent of the chat header's tag filter and of its + button, and 88 percent of the Outline's tag filter and part of its search field; on a desktop it covered the chat tab strip's tag filter and its settings gear. The Feed shows no header above its list, so there the badge sat on the list's sticky column heads, and on a desktop Feed three columns wide it covered part of the third column's drag chip. The controls stayed hidden for as long as the wait lasted. The change keeps the badge's CSS rule byte for byte and places the badge from its script. Its first place is 8 px below the top of the pane's content container (the cid _pane_spin is given) when that container is the pane's scroll area (overflow-y auto or scroll): below the header in the chat, the Outline and the Waiting pane, and upstream's 8 px in the Feed. That place alone still covered controls that do not scroll with the content: the chat's subagent viewer header, a sticky line inside the transcript's container whose 'keep this tab' pin it covered whole; the chat's landing notice, the only cancel for a jump, drawn over the transcript's top from outside its container; and the Feed's column heads with their drag chips. So while the badge is painted it moves to the nearest place that covers no control that stays put when the content scrolls: a control outside the container (the header, and what is drawn over the container from outside it, such as the landing notice and the scroll marks), and inside the container a control that is or sits in a sticky or fixed element. The controls count, not the boxes that hold them, so the badge may sit over a sticky header's text, as it may over the text of the chrome above the list. Each control counts by the part a person can see: its box clipped by every ancestor whose overflow clips it, along its chain of containing blocks (a fixed control escapes every ancestor that is not its containing block, an absolute one the ancestors between it and its positioned containing block). Without the clip, the rows that the desktop chat's tab strip and the pinned-notes strip scroll out of view, which lie over the list's top, pushed the badge down the transcript, or on a short pane onto a visible tab (the fork's second review round). It steps down past what it would cover, or left of it when that is the shorter move (a scroll mark at the right edge); when that walk reaches the container's visible bottom, a second walk from the first place steps left wherever the badge fits with 8 px to spare, so a column of controls through the badge's column cannot send it down onto them while a place to its left is clear. If neither walk finds a clear place, as when the list is shorter than the badge (a landscape phone with the keyboard up and a long pinned note), it takes the place in the pane's view, at the right edge and 8 px above or below one of those controls, that covers the least of them, the nearest its first place among equals. That is a clear place wherever the view's right edge has room for the badge with 8 px to spare above and below, and it may be over the chrome's text above the list; only a view without that room leaves the badge over part of a control, and taps pass through it. Before this the first place stood there: the fork's rehearsed check of its first review round found the badge over the composer's buttons, below a list 20 px tall. A control is a link, button, form field, label or summary, an element with a button role, a tabindex, draggable=true or a data-act action, or anything drawn with a pointer or grab cursor or one of CSS's resize cursors, the cursor keywords that end in -resize (the drag chip, the landing notice and the chat composer's resize handle have only their cursor; the fork's ruling on its second review round: on a landscape phone with the keyboard up and a long pinned note the badge sat over the composer's handle). A cursor counts only where the element sets it, where its cursor differs from its parent's, the body's own children compared with the body's: cursor inherits, and while the composer's or the tab strip's resize handle is dragged the chat sets ns-resize on the body, so every element that sets no cursor of its own, the loader's own full-view sheet among them, read as a control, no place was clear, and the painted badge left its place for the drag (the fork's third review round). An element that only inherits a cursor counts through the element that sets it. RCTL names the two handles, #composer-resize and #tabbar-resize, because during its own drag a handle's cursor equals its parent's. A scrollbar is not a control. Controls in the content itself, the top row of messages or cards, are not avoided: they scroll from under the badge, and taps pass through it. On a phone Feed, whose three heads are stacked and sit close together when the sections are short, the badge goes below the last drag chip that reaches its place. The badge is placed at load and at each ResizeObserver callback on the container and the page (a scroll area's box moves only with a size change), at its own paint, and while it is painted, at the animation frame after a change to the page, a scroll or the end of a CSS transition (the scroll marks move to their places by one, and only its end says where they stop), at most once a frame, so a control that appears or moves under it moves it on; no timer, and nothing is searched while it is down. A ResizeObserver callback while the badge is painted places it at once only when no placement has read the layout the callback reports; in a frame whose own placement already read it (an engine runs the frame's animation frame callbacks before its ResizeObserver callbacks, so a text field that grows a line reaches both in one frame), the callback asks for a search at the next frame instead (rkey, rgeo), so no frame places it twice for one change (the fork's rehearsed check of its second review round). The watch observes the container's own children and attributes, each sticky or fixed element in it, and the page outside the container, not the rest of the container's content, and it reads every element's style again only for a change that can add a control (an element added inside no control the last search held, or a class, style or hidden change on an element the last search did not hold or that holds elements). Each attribute record carries the value the attribute had (attributeOldValue), and a record whose value did not change is skipped and asks for nothing: on a desktop chat each scroll step writes the reply chips' hidden attribute (fixed, outside the list, holding elements) to the value it had, which read every element's style at each step before this rule (the fork's second review round). A text change such as a status timer's tick, an element added inside a control it holds whose box, and the box of each element it holds, lies inside the part of that control a person can see (a chat rewrites its status line's mode icon every second; rin), a style change on a field it holds as the field grows, a scroll and the end of a transition re-read the boxes it found. An element added inside a control it holds that reaches outside the part a person can see, such as a popup child of the control, reads every element's style as before, so a control it brings is found (the fork's ruling on its second review round). Inside means at any depth: the control is the record's target or its nearest ancestor the search holds, because the chat writes its mode icon into a span that only inherits the mode chip's pointer cursor, which under the cursor rule above is no control of its own; read by the target alone, that write read every element's style once a second (the fork's third review round). Before this every change anywhere in the page read every element's style while the badge was painted, which put a whole-page read on each tick and each keystroke of a chat (the fork's second review round). Three changes outside the container measured in the fork's lab still read every element's style while the badge is painted: each keystroke of a slash command, as the slash menu (fixed, outside the list) redraws its items, which are real controls; on a desktop chat each new line in the composer, as the chat writes a new height into the scroll marks' container (outside the list, holding elements), a read the resize the growing composer causes asks for in the same frame, so a new line costs one such read on every layout (the fork's check of its second review round's fixes); and a scroll through a transcript that is not from today, as the chat moves its day label (.rail-day, its style top) and shows or hides its sticky time stamp (.time-marker.rail-sticky, its display) at the handoffs between the rail's stamps, fixed elements outside the list that the last search did not hold. Ten wheel steps of 120 px up from the bottom of the lab's transcript, dated 2024-01-01, gave those writes 2 such reads in Chromium and in Firefox and 4 to 8 in WebKit, beside 1 for the jump button the first step shows (the fork's third review round). They are disclosed rather than spared: a rule that spared a class, style or hidden write on an element the last search did not hold could miss a control that write shows elsewhere through a sibling or :has() selector. The scroll-area test is made at each placement, not once at load: Firefox resolves no computed style in a frame that is not rendered, so in a phone opened on the Feed tab, whose chat iframe loads display:none, overflow-y read as empty at load, and a test made only there left the chat's badge in the corner over the header (the fork's served Firefox leg caught it; Chromium and WebKit resolve the style there). A container that is not a scroll area keeps the corner. Upstream's main carries the same badge rule, the same pane layouts, the subagent viewer's sticky header, the landing notice and the Feed's sticky column heads (read 2026-10-03), so its badge covers the same controls. In the fork a painted badge stays until the pane's first fresh frame on a page with a shell, which made the covered controls matter more there.

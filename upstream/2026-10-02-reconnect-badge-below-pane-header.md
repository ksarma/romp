The corner badge that says reconnecting over a pane with content (T217) sits at top:8px, right:8px. In the chat, the Outline and the Waiting pane that is over the right end of the header above the pane's list. In a hermetic lab on the fork, on a phone it covered 78 percent of the chat header's tag filter and of its + button, and 88 percent of the Outline's tag filter and part of its search field; on a desktop it covered the chat tab strip's tag filter and its settings gear. The Feed shows no header above its list, so there the badge sat on the list's sticky column heads, and on a desktop Feed three columns wide it covered part of the third column's drag chip. The controls stayed hidden for as long as the wait lasted. The change keeps the badge's CSS rule byte for byte and places the badge from its script. Its first place is 8 px below the top of the pane's content container (the cid _pane_spin is given) when that container is the pane's scroll area (overflow-y auto or scroll): below the header in the chat, the Outline and the Waiting pane, and upstream's 8 px in the Feed. That place alone still covered controls that do not scroll with the content: the chat's subagent viewer header, a sticky line inside the transcript's container whose 'keep this tab' pin it covered whole; the chat's landing notice, the only cancel for a jump, drawn over the transcript's top from outside its container; and the Feed's column heads with their drag chips. So while the badge is painted it moves to the nearest place that covers nothing which stays put when the content scrolls and holds a control: a control outside the container (the header, and what is drawn over the container from outside it, such as the landing notice and the scroll marks), and inside the container the whole box of a sticky or fixed element that is or holds a control, so the badge goes below such a header, never onto it. It steps down past what it would cover, or left of it when that is the shorter move (a scroll mark at the right edge). If no place above the container's visible bottom is clear, as when the list is shorter than the badge (a landscape phone with the keyboard up and a long pinned note), it takes the place in the pane's view, at the right edge and 8 px above or below one of those controls, that covers the least of them, the nearest its first place among equals. That is a clear place wherever the view's right edge has room for the badge with 8 px to spare above and below, and it may be over the chrome's text above the list; only a view without that room leaves the badge over part of a control, and taps pass through it. Before this the first place stood there: the fork's rehearsed check of its first review round found the badge over the composer's buttons, below a list 20 px tall. A control is a link, button, form field, label or summary, an element with a button role, a tabindex, draggable=true or a data-act action, or anything drawn with a pointer or grab cursor (the drag chip and the landing notice have only their cursor). Controls in the content itself, the top row of messages or cards, are not avoided: they scroll from under the badge, and taps pass through it. On a phone Feed, whose three heads are stacked and sit close together when the sections are short, the badge goes below the last of them that reaches its place. The badge is placed at load and at each ResizeObserver callback on the container and the page (a scroll area's box moves only with a size change), at its own paint, and while it is painted, at each change to the page's elements (a MutationObserver on the body) and each scroll, so a control that appears or moves under it moves it on; no timer, and nothing is searched while it is down. The scroll-area test is made at each placement, not once at load: Firefox resolves no computed style in a frame that is not rendered, so in a phone opened on the Feed tab, whose chat iframe loads display:none, overflow-y read as empty at load, and a test made only there left the chat's badge in the corner over the header (the fork's served Firefox leg caught it; Chromium and WebKit resolve the style there). A container that is not a scroll area keeps the corner. Upstream's main carries the same badge rule, the same pane layouts, the subagent viewer's sticky header, the landing notice and the Feed's sticky column heads (read 2026-10-03), so its badge covers the same controls. In the fork a painted badge stays until the pane's first fresh frame on a page with a shell, which made the covered controls matter more there.

An offer to the project carries the placement (rplace, rfit, rcov, robs, rwatch and their observers), its node cases and the served check of each painted badge's box against the controls that stay put, with the three surface legs (the subagent viewer leg needs the lab's synthetic subagent, which the served module seeds). The Outline's outage-tap leg is fork-only: it taps a pane the fork's shim parked. The guide sentence it touches sits in the fork's phone paragraph, which the project does not have, so an offer leaves docs/guide.md out.

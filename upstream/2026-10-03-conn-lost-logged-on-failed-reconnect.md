Every return to the app logged "Kernel connection lost" for the chat and the Feed and left an unread digit on the Log control, although the sockets came back at once. The Log wrote its entry at each pane's up-to-down transition, and every return makes one: the shim puts a stale socket down before it redials, and a thawed page receives the FIN of a socket the OS dropped. Now the transition records the drop in the Log's lost, and the entry is written when the drop's reconnect fails: the close of a dial that never opened, the pane's own (the shim's new wsFail word, posted after that close's down word) or, since the panes wait on the shell's link at a return, the shell's own (window.__rompLinkFailed from its socket's close). A socket that reopens first drops the waiting entry unwritten. The Log keeps no timer of its own, but a dial's connect cut is one (15 s), and a close the cut made fails nothing while another socket of the page is open. The kernel is answering that socket, and Chromium and Firefox hold each WebSocket handshake to a host until the one ahead of it is done; on a slow network the last of the dials a return makes together can reach its cut while every handshake is succeeding. A close made by the page's own unload fails nothing: Firefox closes a page's dials that never opened after beforeunload and before pagehide and delivers those closes to the page, so a reload while a dial was connecting wrote one entry per shown pane. A latch set on beforeunload stops both failure doors, and the shell's next dial, the next frame on its link, a socket's open or pageshow clears it, since beforeunload also fires for a navigation that does not unload (a 204, a download). In Chromium and WebKit a 204 closes no socket, so through an outage of the panes alone the link's frame is the one clear (the kernel's keepalive comes every 10 s); a frame that lands between an unload's beforeunload and the closes Firefox delivers mostly 1 to 2 ms later (453 of 462 within 13 ms, the latest at 21 ms) clears the latch too, and that reload writes an entry for each shown pane whose dial the unload closed. A refused dial fails whatever is open; a hung outage, with nothing open, is written at its first cut, at 15 s, while the live red cue shows the drop from the start. A pane not shown keeps its drop waiting and is written at a failure once it is shown; a parked pane's drop is dropped. Upstream's main writes the entry at the same transition (read 2026-10-03), so a return there logs it too; upstream has no shell link, so its port is the shim's word and the Log's lost, stands and leaving latch, without the shell's calls (there a pane's open and pageshow clear the latch); its panes dial together at a return, so a slow network queues their handshakes there too. Upstream's visibility path closes a CONNECTING socket with its handlers attached, so the port must first put that socket down as abandon() does (handlers detached, ws nulled, redial at once), or that close posts a failure on a healthy return. The port needs two more things. First, a clear of its own for the latch, since upstream has no shell link: with only a pane's open and pageshow to clear it, the first outage after any 204 (and, untested, any download) goes unwritten in Chromium and WebKit. Those engines close no socket at a 204, so the outage's refusals arrive while the latch is set, and the panes' opens at its end clear the latch and drop the waiting entries together. For that clear a port can use upstream's app=shell socket: it receives the kernel's keepalive, so its onmessage could make the port's clear, as the shell link's frames do here. Second, the ported wsFail listener needs upstream's fail-closed __rompPaneSourceOk(e) check as its first statement, which every upstream shell listener for window messages has carried since d1ccedaf0 (read on upstream/main at f4a572008).

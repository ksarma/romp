Handler._send adds Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin to every response it builds, and a send_error override adds it once to the refusals http.server writes itself: the 414 for a request line over 65536 bytes, which a page on another origin can open as a top-level document with a long URL, the two 431s, the 501, and the 400 for a request line of four or more words whose last is a well-formed version below HTTP/2.0 other than HTTP/0.9 itself, and, from Python 3.13.15 and 3.14.7, the 400 or 505 for any other request line http.server refuses as malformed. A page on another origin that opens a dashboard page with window.open keeps a handle to it, and the handle is what lets that page post it window messages; under same-origin a top-level document the kernel serves starts a browsing context group of its own, so that opener gets a closed handle and the page has no opener. The dashboard's own tabs (a /file image or PDF it opens with window.open) are documents on its origin with the same policy, so window.open still returns a handle and the in-app view does not take over (preview.ts openFileTab reads only whether it got one). Browsers enforce the policy only on secure contexts (https, localhost) and read it on document navigations only; on a plain-http tailnet address it is ignored, and the sender checks in the pages' own listeners (upstream/2026-09-24-chat-handler-foreign-sender.md) are the defence everywhere.

The override sets a flag around super().send_error(), and end_headers adds the header only while the flag is set, since send_error writes its headers there and _send writes its own copy: a response with two copies of the header leaves a browser with no policy. Not covered: a reply in HTTP/0.9's shape, with no status line and no headers at all. Only a request line of at most 65536 bytes, its line terminator included, gets one, and no browser sends such a line. On Python 3.10 to 3.12, and on 3.13 and 3.14 before 3.13.15 and 3.14.7, a line whose version is missing, malformed (a word after the version makes it so), HTTP/0.9 itself, or HTTP/2.0 or later gets every reply in that shape: http.server's refusals (its 400 and 505 for the line, and its 431s and 501 for a line that gets past those) and the kernel's own reply to the request alike. From 3.13.15 and 3.14.7, the first 3.13 and 3.14 releases with CPython's gh-54930, http.server's 400 and 505 for such a line have a status line and carry the policy, and the shape is left to a line of two words whose first is GET and a line of three whose version is HTTP/0.9 itself. On all of them an authorized GET (`GET /chat?token=<token>`, say) gets the page itself, a bare `GET /` the sign-in page, and `GET /chat` with no credential the gate's refusal, each with no headers. A longer line is not among them: http.server writes the 414 before it parses the line, so that line gets the full 414, the policy included, whatever version it names. So the 431s and the 501 carry the policy only on a request line whose version is below HTTP/2.0 and not HTTP/0.9, which gets a full reply, the policy included. The responses written with send_response directly (the /file and relay HEAD and range replies, the downloads, the CORS preflight and the socket upgrade) carry no policy; none of them is a document.

fix tier: tests/test_kernel_auth_hardening.py OpenerIsolation reads the header off a live socket, bare and under each navigation's request headers, session cookie and, on /file, cap (tests/document_navigations.py), on the shell, every pane page, the sign-in page on / and on /login, an asset and each document shape the /file route builds, and requires exactly one copy; test_each_refusal_http_server_writes_itself_carries_it_once reads each of the six refusals once off the wire, with no navigation's headers or cookie, and requires exactly one copy: the 414, both 431s, the 501 and two 400s (a request line ending in HTTP/1.1 and one ending in HTTP/0.5), the 431s and the 400s over a raw socket; test_a_request_line_over_65536_bytes_gets_the_full_414_whatever_its_version reads the 414 to a line over 65536 bytes, its terminator included, in seven version forms, among them the forms that get replies in HTTP/0.9's shape when the line is shorter, and to the shortest such line ending in CRLF (65535 bytes of text), and requires one copy on each; test_a_request_line_no_browser_sends_gets_no_headers_at_all_or_the_policy_once sends seventeen of the shorter lines and judges each reply by its shape, never by the interpreter's version: with no status line, the body the line gets must start at the reply's first byte, and with a status line, the status must be the one that body goes with and the header must be there once; it sends six of those requests, ones the kernel answers itself, on HTTP/0.5, which gets a full reply, and requires one copy on each; tests/test_kernel_remote_file_relay.py covers every shape the /remote relay answers through _send; OpenerIsolationWireWitness shows that a second copy written in send_response or in end_headers reaches the wire as two. Without the header line in _send, OpenerIsolation, the relay's opener tests and the witness fail; without the override, the refusal test fails on each of its six refusals and the 414 test on each of its eight lines, and on 3.13.15 and 3.14.7 the shape test fails on each of the six lines those interpreters refuse with a status line. In Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, a page on another origin that opens the 414 gets a closed handle and the error page has no opener; without the override the handle stays live and the page keeps its opener.

Upstream's main (f4a572008) sends no opener policy, and its Handler has no send_error or end_headers: log_message is followed by _send. Here end_headers is one method with fork PR #919's legacy-cookie clear, the send_error block follows #919's handle_one_request, parse_request and _clears_legacy_cookie, and _send's opener line sits above #919's Referrer-Policy comment. Upstream has none of these, so only the hunk that adds the _in_send_error flag applies there as it is. On upstream the change adds end_headers whole (the flag test and its header, then super().end_headers()), puts the send_error block and end_headers after log_message, and puts the opener line ahead of upstream's own Referrer-Policy comment, the form the change had before the merge of main that brought #919 in.

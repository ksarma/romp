Upstream's test (romp-on/romp pull 1763) pins the /perf heap block's read to a median under 5 ms over populated caches, thirty times a fresh lab process's 166 us. That pin holds the process, not the block: the allocatedBlocks gauge, sys.getallocatedblocks(), walks the allocator's live heap on every build (CPython Objects/obmalloc.c). On the GIL builds pymalloc walks every pool of every live arena, O(live heap): measured on 3.12.3, 2.8 ms at 3 GB live and 5.7 ms at 6 GB, the resident size the lag investigation cited for a live kernel, so a large kernel's /perf poll pays about 5 ms in that one read and the 5 ms pin is a latent red on the GIL cells as a serial suite grows. On a free-threaded build the walk covers every thread state's mimalloc heaps and then the abandoned pool once per heap tag, where exited threads' segments stay after their objects are freed: the fork's serial 3.14t CI cell (PYTHON_GIL=0, one pytest process; upstream's CI has 3.10 to 3.13 cells only) read a median of 5.451 ms after about 10,000 tests while the module alone reads 120 us. The free-threaded walk is not the slower one as such: at this module's place in the suite's alphabetical prefix, with 8 live threads and a 3.4 to 3.9 GB resident size, it read 1.10 ms on 3.14.6t against 1.17 ms on 3.12.3, so the cost is the live heap's on every build plus the thread residue on the free-threaded one. Every other gauge in the block costs 0.1 to 1.5 us on both builds and the block without that read 7 to 8 us. The fork's change is test-side plus one docstring line: the case times an empty-cache read of the block first, in the same process, and holds the populated read to READ_MULTIPLE (40) times it, so both reads pay the allocator's walk and the ratio cancels it, no build carries a wall number, and what the case guards (no gauge walks a populated cache's entries) shows in the populated read alone: a probe re-deriving hydrated.bytes over the 100,000 entries read ratios of 151 to 238 on both builds, red, where its 3.0 ms would have passed the 5 ms pin. The multiple comes from one box's medians of 20 (2026-09-17): a fresh process reads 6.7 to 8.1 on 3.12 and 9.1 to 11.2 on 3.14t, five other modules first 3.3 and 3.9, 2 GB allocated and freed first 3.4 on 3.12, the CI floor about 1.02, so a fresh process is the worst case and 40 is about 3.5 times the largest ratio it read. The bare walk is still timed beside the read and printed in the case's line and its failure message. kernel/kernel.py's _heap_stats docstring, which called every gauge O(1) or O(entries), names allocatedBlocks as O(live heap) now. The test's name stays and nothing retires. Any runner of upstream's suite with a large live heap or a free-threaded build meets the same floor, so the relative bound with its header and the docstring line are the offer. Filed by the 2026-09-17 catch-up fold (fixer round c4 took a build-gated 50 ms bound as a stopgap, review round 1 item 4 replaced it with the relative bound); the offer waits on the no-new-upstreaming hold like the 2026-09-16 entries, the ledger being the queue.

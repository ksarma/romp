Upstream's test (romp-on/romp pull 1763) pins the /perf heap block's read to a median under 5 ms over populated caches, thirty times a fresh lab process's 166 us. That pin holds the process, not the block: the allocatedBlocks gauge, sys.getallocatedblocks(), walks the allocator's live heap on every build (CPython Objects/obmalloc.c). On the GIL builds pymalloc walks every pool of every live arena, O(live heap): measured on 3.12.3, 2.8 ms at 3 GB live and 5.7 ms at 6 GB, the resident size the lag investigation cited for a live kernel, so a large kernel's /perf poll pays about 5 ms in that one read and the 5 ms pin is a latent red on the GIL cells as a serial suite grows. On a free-threaded build the walk covers every thread state's mimalloc heaps and then the abandoned pool once per heap tag, where exited threads' segments stay after their objects are freed: the fork's serial 3.14t CI cell (PYTHON_GIL=0, one pytest process; upstream's CI has 3.10 to 3.13 cells only) read a median of 5.451 ms after about 10,000 tests while the module alone reads 120 us. The free-threaded walk is not the slower one as such: at this module's place in the suite's alphabetical prefix, with 8 live threads and a 3.4 to 3.9 GB resident size, it read 1.10 ms on 3.14.6t against 1.17 ms on 3.12.3, so the cost is the live heap's on every build plus the thread residue on the free-threaded one. Every other gauge in the block costs 0.1 to 1.5 us on both builds and the block without that read 7 to 8 us. The fork's change is test-side plus one docstring line: the case EXCLUDES the walk from both timed reads, swapping the kernel module's sys for a stub whose getallocatedblocks answers a constant (the idiom the module's AbsentSupport case already uses: a SimpleNamespace carrying stderr and exc_info, which _heap_read's said-once line writes through), so the timed reads hold the gauges alone, the work the case exists to bound; the primary assertion is an absolute wall of 2 ms over that (READ_WALL_S), the populated read against an empty-cache read timed first in the same process (READ_MULTIPLE, 60) the secondary one, and the bare walk is timed apart and printed in the case's line and both failure messages, so a red names its share of a real read. A first fix (review round 1) held the populated read to 40 times the empty-cache read with both reads paying the walk, the ratio meant to cancel it; review round 2 showed that ratio blind exactly where it was wanted: once the walk dominates both reads, as on the serial cell the change was made for, a gauge that walks the 100,000 populated entries adds about 3 ms to a base the walk has raised and passes (a mutant re-deriving hydrated.bytes over the entries, in a scratch copy of the kernel: red at 118 to 269 times in a fresh process, green at 2.6 times on 3.12 and 12.7 times on 3.14t with 40 million live objects staged first; the review's own probes read 266 to 420 fresh and 3.5 to 33 staged). With the walk excluded the timed reads are the same in every process state, on one box's medians of 20 (2026-09-17): the gauges over the populated caches 96 to 112 us and the empty-cache read 6 to 8 us on both builds, fresh, after five other modules in the same process, and with the 40 million live objects staged (the walk itself 10 us to 2.5 ms across those states), ratios 12.6 to 19.5; the same mutant reads 2.9 to 4.2 ms, 383 to 552 times, red under the wall in every state. READ_WALL_S is about 20 times the reading and READ_MULTIPLE about three times the largest ratio read. kernel/kernel.py's _heap_stats docstring, which called every gauge O(1) or O(entries), names allocatedBlocks as O(live heap) and says the read-cost test excludes that walk and bounds the other gauges alone. The test's name stays and nothing retires. Any runner of upstream's suite with a large live heap or a free-threaded build meets the same floor, so the exclusion with its header and the docstring line are the offer. Filed by the 2026-09-17 catch-up fold (fixer round c4 took a build-gated 50 ms bound as a stopgap, review round 1 item 4 replaced it with a relative bound, review round 2 item 2 replaced the relative bound with the exclusion and the wall); the offer waits on the no-new-upstreaming hold like the 2026-09-16 entries, the ledger being the queue.

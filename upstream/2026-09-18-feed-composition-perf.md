The feed frame (about 8.8 MB on a busy board) goes whole to the feed pane, the Outline and the Waiting-on-you pane, and nothing said what it was made of. memos.feedComposition now carries, per per-entry encode of a frame (the pusher's send stage, the handler-thread cold serve and the cold kernel's first frame), the bytes by component for the last pass (lifetime sums over every pass are kept in the store and not published; the privacy paragraph says why): `frame`, `cards` (the per-card strings) and `rest` (the frame outside the cards: the per-ledger strings and the remainder), the last of them split by top-level field into the flag and count rows and one `other` row that holds the ledgers and every field whose value can carry a string, folded at report time; and per consuming app the bytes it receives today beside what it would receive if sent only the fields its bundle reads (a checked-in table pinned against the webview source; the projections count the folded fields as one and the Outline's card-field estimate is published as its row's `cardFields`, so each is a sum of published rows). The card count and the ledger count are measured and withheld, and the ledgers have no row of their own; the privacy paragraph says why. The remainder is encoded per field with one encoder and joined into the same sort_keys string, byte for byte; no second encode, no frame change. The card-field and projection estimators run inside their own guard: a raise is counted under `failed`, said once, memoised with the cards so a later hit does not re-raise, and the frame goes out unchanged at every call site. Beside the readers' rows, a phoneFace projection row sizes a frame that does not exist yet, a phone client's feed slot carrying a face per active card (address, title, state, age) plus one summary row per session with a card, so the gap to the phone's cold-open goal is measured before the face frame is designed. Landing is the user's call whatever the tier label, since the block widens what the export carries by kind.

Privacy, in one paragraph. A per-field `by` table published the length of one string for several rows: `selfHost` was sixteen plus the length of the machine's short hostname on every board, a number derived from the machine's name that the export's identifier scan cannot see because it reads strings, not numbers; `working`, `awaiting` and `stateUnknown` were a per-field constant (seventeen for `working`) plus one session name's length when one name was listed; `userTodoRows`, `syncNotices` and `sdkNotices` were the length of one todo's text (which can name an absolute path) or one notice's prose when the board held one; and with the session count recoverable from `order`, `sessions` gave the total length of the session names and repository strings, up to a bounded per-session unknown (a null or fixed-width colour, a null or quoted repository). Dropping or coarsening one row would not have closed this, because `rest` equals the sum of the table exactly, so the same number was re-derivable from the remainder. The fix folds the table at report time by a pinned, checked-in list (FEED_BY_FOLDED), classified by what a field can carry rather than by a fixture's value (judgeLimit included: null normally, but session and host names when the latch is down): ledgers, selfHost, working, awaiting, stateUnknown, order, sessions, userTodos, userTodoRows, views, viewsFault, judgeLimit, bgServices, clearedForeign, clearNotices, sdkNotices and syncNotices go into one `other` row; the published rows are the five flag and count fields (FEED_BY_ROWS: userTodosOn, dismissedCount, showDismissed, canUndoClear, off), the off frame's four empty federation lists (items, hosts, pendingHosts, pendingDead) and `other`, a fixed set whatever the board; `frame` is `cards` plus `rest` and `rest` is the exact sum of the published table; a key outside FEED_FRAME_FIELDS and the off frame's lists is counted under `other`, so a future field cannot ride into the export by name; the stored tables stay unfolded (the fold is at report time, in one place), and the per-app projections read them but count the folded fields as one: an app that reads any folded field is credited with all of `other`, and only the flag and count rows it reads are added by name. The invariant, in the words of the kernel's FEED_COMPOSITION_INVARIANT and of the reference (a test holds the three equal): Every published number is a published row or a sum of published rows: `frame` is `cards` plus `rest`; `rest` is the sum of the `by` table; every `today` is `frame`; the feed row is `cards` plus `other` plus the flag rows it reads; the Outline's row is its `cardFields`, the card-field estimate published beside it, plus `other` plus `off`; the Waiting-on-you row is `other` plus `userTodosOn`; and a one-character step in any folded field, the ledgers among them, moves the same published leaves by the same amounts, whichever field took it, so no published number or difference of published numbers says which folded field a byte belongs to. The estimate is a row since the third round: the round before withheld it on the ground that on a one-card board it is that card's field lengths, which was no ground, since the Outline's row minus `other` and `off` gave the same number on every board; the row is an aggregate over the cards on the footing of `cards`, whose count is withheld and recoverable nowhere on /perf, so whether it stands is the same question the second residual leaves to the user. The one-atom rule closed a second re-derivation the review found: with the projections published as exact per-field sums beside frame, ledgers and the flag rows, frame minus ledgers minus the feed row minus userTodosOn was the userTodoRows row and the Waiting-on-you row minus that minus userTodosOn the sessions row, on every board, so on a one-session board with no repository string the session name's length was published. The third round added the counts and the ledgers. The first version published a card count and a ledger count beside the `cards` and `ledgers` sums; a count published beside a sum discloses the single-object case, because a sum over one object is that object's measurement and the count says when, so a sum is an aggregate only while its count is unpublished, in the block or anywhere else in the export. Both counts are now measured, kept in the stored tables and withheld from the published ones. That is enough for the cards, whose count is published nowhere else on /perf, so `cards` stands as an aggregate. It is not enough for the ledgers: their count is the chat tab count, one ledger row per built or provisional tab, and /perf publishes that count whatever this block does (heap.builtChat.tabs, caches.built_chat.entries, memos.chatLedger.entries and the length of builds.chat.bySession are each the tab count on a steady board), so a published `ledgers` was one session's whole ledger row (a constant plus its name, its status spelling and its outline text) on a one-session board, beside a count the reader already had. The ledgers therefore join the fold, and the remainder invariant re-checked after that fold set the published shape: with the ledgers folded but frame, cards and the remainder published, frame minus cards minus the remainder was the ledgers again, the table's sum minus the remainder was the ledgers again, and frame minus the feed row minus userTodosOn was the ledgers again, on every board. So the published `rest` is the frame outside the cards, `other` carries the ledgers, and every app that reads a folded field is credited with the atom, the ledgers included: the feed and Waiting-on-you rows over-count by the ledgers they do not read, and the reference says so. The same re-check across passes removed the lifetime table from the published block: the cold kernel's first push with a feed pane connected counts the cards-first frame without ledgers and then the send stage's refill of the same build with them, so with `passes` published as 2 the lifetime table and `last` were two exact sums over passes sharing a build and twice `last.rest` minus `lifetime.rest` was the ledgers again; no lifetime sum survives that subtraction while a pass can share its build with the pass before it, so the block publishes `passes`, `failed`, `last` and `wire`, and the lifetime sums stay in the store for the tests (a test drives the cold sequence on the real pusher and holds the row's length to no published leaf, difference, sum or 2a - b). The rows over-count the fields an app reads by the rest of `other`; a test steps every folded field by one character, a ledger's text among them, and holds the set of moved leaves and their deltas equal across the fields, over every integer leaf of the block, and a one-session board with a ledger attached is held to no published number or difference of published numbers being that ledger row's length. Two residuals remain, stated here in the words of the kernel's FEED_COMPOSITION_RESIDUALS and of the reference (a test holds the three equal). First: On a board with no session, no open todo, no tag, no notice, no cleared id, no judge-limit latch, an empty stored session order and a clean tags read, `other` is a constant plus the hostname's length and the digit width of `views.seq`, which the frame's whole length on `push.send` and the served body has always carried; with a session it is the sum of that session's name and id, its ledger row when the ledgers are attached, the pips, the tag names and the notices, and no published number or difference of published numbers is one of those alone; two blocks served across a change differ by what changed, as the frame's length on `push.send` always did. Second, for the user's ruling: The card figures are aggregates over the cards, whose count is withheld and published nowhere else on /perf (the feed's view-delta split, the path a ?delta=1 client without the feed delta capability takes, counted one entry per card per build under memos.wire until the review's third round and counts none now): `cards` is the whole per-card strings, and the two card-field estimates (the Outline's `cardFields`, which is its row minus `other` and `off`; the `phoneFace` row) are sums of a few fields' lengths over the cards, so on a board with one card `cards` is that card's string and `cardFields` its title, name, summary, background and blockSummary lengths plus a constant and the digit width of its id, and on a board with one active card the `phoneFace` row is a constant plus that card's title length, the constant fixed by the column's spelling and the width of `t`. The `phoneFace` row moves for a card's title and for none of the other texts a person writes (a summary, a background, a session name, a note), so a title step is told from every other step; and a session's name rides in each of its cards and in the folded fields, so two blocks served across a one-character rename move `cards` by that session's card count and `other` by the number of folded fields carrying the name. A reader bounds the count from the size of `cards` (a card's fixed keys are several hundred bytes), from the `phoneFace` row's group rows (61 bytes per session holding fewer than ten cards, so the number of sessions with a card is exact when no card is active) and, while `wire.exact` is 1, from `wire.bytes` minus `frame` (the key names, the separators and one tint per card and per tree node, about twenty bytes each), so a board whose one card has no tree shows as one card.

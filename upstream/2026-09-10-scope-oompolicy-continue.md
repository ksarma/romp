Both halves of fork PR scope-oompolicy-continue, as one fix. Upstream carries the scope wrapper (their #953, landed as b570ef491) with the limits-only OOMPolicy line (their #1008, landed as 30be58404), so every limit-free session scope there keeps systemd's default `stop`, and one OOM-killed tool child ends the whole session (41 background tasks at once on this fork, 2026-09-10). The wrapper half puts `OOMPolicy=continue` on every scope, and the kernel's boot probe settles the policy apart from the memory limits: a systemd before 253 refuses the property and takes the limits, so the limits are probed alone once more and stand, the marker `ROMP_CLI_SCOPE_OOM_POLICY_REJECTED` goes down on every launch with an explicit value, and only a refused limit is a problem line. The heal half: when a session's CLI dies mid-turn, the heal asks systemd about the exact scope unit that CLI ran in (recorded from its /proc/<pid>/cgroup at connect) and reads three signals, primary first: the CLI's own exit (a SIGKILL is the OOM killer's signature under `continue`); the cgroup's memory.events oom_kill count against the turn's baseline (read for the sigkill kind, where a flat counter names a kill the counter did not see, and to tell a contained child kill apart); and Result=oom-kill for a scope without the property (third because under `continue` systemd records no Result), with the journal standing in once the unit is collected: its `Failed with result 'oom-kill'` line for the whole-scope stop, and for a SIGKILL exit its `A process of this unit has been killed by the OOM killer` line, which survives the collection and tells the OOM killer from a kill by hand. It then names the OOM kill in the kernel log and queues an out-of-memory form of the crash resume notice instead of the bare one. An upstream port also needs the consumer at their kernel/sdk_backend.py that keeps a nudge at the queue head by equality with CRASH_RESUME_NUDGE widened to is_crash_resume_nudge, the heal's own queue de-dup filter widened the same way, and their test_kernel_interrupt_machine_cut.py source pin (`reg["queue"] = [CRASH_RESUME_NUDGE]`) moved to the `nudge` pick. Round 3 (2026-09-10): a SIGKILL whose scope counter was read and did not rise over the turn's baseline is named a kill the counter did not see (a third form of the notice, CRASH_RESUME_NUDGE_KILLED), never out of memory; a collected unit has its journal tail read, since the Result leaves with the unit; the SDK's -1 exit reads as unreported and 137 as a plain exit; the heal names a memory limit only when one is set and not known to be unapplied, in the boot line's words, and the loop top no longer reads the old scope's counter at a reconnect. Round 4 (2026-09-10): the SIGKILL + collected cell is decided by the journal's kill line or stop line (oom with either, the hedged kill with neither or with no readable record); the heal's scope reads run before the dead session is popped, so a send in those milliseconds lands behind the nudge; the journal read keeps the manager's own lines (`-t systemd`) and this life's (from the last Started line). Round 5 (2026-09-10): the killed notice is one text for every no-record cell (a kill by signal 9, no out-of-memory kill on record, which does not rule one out); systemd-oomd's `systemd-oomd killed N process(es) in this unit` line is a third kill line and files oom; a journal with no line for the unit reads as no record, not as the flat-counter silence; from the cut the dying session's queue mirror is sealed and the heal's write folds its in-memory pending behind the nudge and closes the queue, so a send that reaches the dying session during the reads lands in the reg and one that reaches it after the fold is refused and re-resolves to the replacement (a send under the crash-loop refusal is parked in the reg and named in the line); every kernel-thread call on a session's loop is guarded against the closed loop, so a user end during the reads raises nothing; the journal window is cut at any Started line, whatever the manager's StatusUnitFormat=. Round 6 (2026-09-10): the heal decides its verdict, attempt count and nudge before the pop and writes the sealed queue and pops the session in one hold of the backend lock (the resume, the crash-loop refusal and the ended-during-the-reads path alike), so a send in the pop-to-write gap resolves the dying session or the written reg, never a pre-write reg; the fold's write lands inside the hold that closes the queue; one helper (_enqueue_resolving) re-resolves a closed queue for send, deliver and the rewind's edit text, where deliver had dropped a peer's mail and reported it delivered; a reg that exists but would not read skips the sealed queue write with the heal's line saying so, instead of a gutted {sid, queue} reg; a stop click during the heal's reads is refused with no idle record over the cut marker. Round 7 (2026-09-10): the rewind arm refuses a sealed or closed queue instead of re-resolving its edit to the replacement, whose queue the crash nudge heads (the edit would ride the rewound branch behind the nudge, and a refused --resume-session-at would pop the nudge as the held edit); the bg-task death notice skips a reg that exists but would not read instead of rebuilding it as {sid, queue, bgTasks} and spawning from it; the re-resolve helper logs the verb it serves.

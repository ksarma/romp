Upstream https://github.com/romp-on/romp/pull/1017 sets everConnected at the first onopen and dials reconnect=1 on every redial, so a fresh page whose first socket died before its bundle loaded declares a redial for sessions it never held; upstream masks it with an unconditional pop at the bundle's ready, the fork's redial flag at accept did not. The fold's dial term is everConnected&&bundleReady&&!readyQueued (round 6; round 5's two-bit term left the queued-ready shape open): the redial declares itself only when the page has opened a socket before, its bundle has said ready, and that ready is not still waiting in the shim's queue for an open. The gate is correct for upstream as well: a redial before the bundle evaluated holds nothing, and a redial whose ready is still queued has not put a listening bundle in front of the kernel either. The residual, stated in the accept-time comment and not claimed closed: a ready that left on an open socket the kernel never processed, the socket dying before any frame came back, still redials with the term; no shim bit sees it, and the page is served skeleton tabs that fill on click or the idle prefetch.

Upstream's shim at 459e50d6 has no bundleReady, readyQueued or READY_GATE_CAP (0 hits in kernel/kernel.py there; it never took the fork's kernel-side ready hold or the onopen re-send of the handshake, and their PR 952 did not carry the readyGate layer, so the feed-deltas entry's merged status overstates that part). The offer is therefore not the one-term change: it carries the two declarations (50810), send()'s bundleReady set and readyQueued set (51049, 51051) and onopen's readyQueued clear after the flush (50984) beside the term. A term-only port throws ReferenceError at the first redial rather than evaluating a silently false term: everConnected is true by then, the term is evaluated inside the WebSocket constructor's argument, and connect() has no try around it (its one try wraps the localStorage read), so the socket is never constructed and no onclose redials again; the first dial short-circuits on everConnected and hides the fault. The hook is valid upstream: acquireVsCodeApi().postMessage routes to send(m) there as here (42053 there, 51127 here), so the bundle's ready passes through send(). The port needs readyQueued as well as bundleReady, verified against upstream's shim: its send() (41977) queues a ready handed to it while the socket is down and its onopen (41922) flushes the queue on the next open, so a bundleReady set at the top of send() would dial the redial with the term while the queued ready flushes on the new socket, the same over-declaration on the queued sub-shape. What upstream lacks is only the fork's onopen re-send (flushedReady) and the kernel hold; the offer needs neither.

The fold also qualified five text sites that stated the pre-gate condition: kernel/kernel.py:44263 and :60187 (the definitional glosses: opened a socket before and its bundle has said ready, with no ready waiting in its queue) and ui/webview/skeleton-tabs.ts:4, tests/test_chat_skeleton_reconnect.py:8 and docs/read-side.md:139 (the three sites that said every redial declares itself, now a redial after the bundle's ready; read-side.md keeps its second clause on what the kernel does). All five are byte-identical to upstream at 459e50d6 (kernel/kernel.py 37268 and 49782, ui/webview/skeleton-tabs.ts:4, the test module's line 8, docs/read-side.md:94 there), so the offer carries them and the next fold meets them at those lines.

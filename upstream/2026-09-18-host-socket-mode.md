asyncio.start_unix_server binds and listens at the umask mode, and the chmod to 0600 landed one line later, so hosts/<sid8>.sock stood at the umask mode for the gap: the last member of the create-then-tighten class PR 789 closed for the kernel credential files, found in its round 1 (fresh-5) and split out as its own fix. Behind the owner-only state root, the one guard then, so a window, not a live hole; the umask is not a guard, since the live session host runs at 002, which leaves the group write bit an AF_UNIX connect needs (an earlier draft of this entry named it as one). The fix checks the published path's length against the socket path budget first (sun_path less its NUL, 107 on Linux: a published path over it is one the kernel can never connect to, and the bind's own check moved onto the temp's name once the temp had a name of its own, so at exactly 108 bytes a first cut published an unreachable socket and logged socket-ready), binds a writer-unique temp name beside the published one (the pid and random digits, the published name's length: the published path is the budget on a deep test root, and a fixed temp name was a shared name two hosts for one sid could cross), tightens it by path (fchmod is a no-op on a bound AF_UNIX socket on Linux), and renames it onto the published path, which is therefore born 0600. During its life at the umask mode the temp has one guard, the mode of hosts/, which both creators (the kernel's write_spawn_spec and the host's bind) make and keep 0700 by code through one helper, owner_only_dir, refusing a symlink or a foreign owner and reading a tighten back; each session's hosts/<sid>/ goes through the same helper on both of its creators (the spawn spec and the host's journal), where round 1 had left it on a bare mkdir. The helper closes the static shape; a re-point between its read-back and the path-taking open that follows needs descriptor-relative calls, and the reachable cases need a state root that is not 0700 (ours is, by code). Every failure on the road is loud and leaves nothing it can vouch for: a socket-bind-failed row with the step, the error class, errno and the path length (never the text, which carries the path), the CLI ended, the lease removed once that CLI is confirmed gone by pid and start-time identity (kept, with a row, while it is not: the kernel's orphan road waits on it, and the transport's word is no proof), exit 1. The kernel connects to the same documented path and no process-wide umask moves. Upstream main carries both halves: its host binds the published path the same way (kernel/session_host.py, start_unix_server then chmod), and both of its roads create hosts/ at the umask's mode (the spawn spec's mkdir with parents=True and the host's own). Round 3 (the reviewer's ruling, 2026-09-19) moved the road's prelude ahead of the CLI: the lease-to-socket interval the kernel's lease-keyed attach roads race had grown from the base's median of about 85 us to about 292 us, because the directory work (hosts/ made ours, the budget check, the dead socket's unlink, the stale-temp sweep, whose cost is proportional to the entries in hosts/) ran between the lease write and the bind; it now runs before the CLI is spawned, so only the bind, the chmod and the rename follow the lease, and a prelude refusal starts no CLI and writes no lease. Measured cold through the real launcher, 40 launches per tree per pass, two passes: the interval's median is 80.7 and 73.1 us on the base, 267.3 and 261.1 on the round-2 head, 107.5 and 100.7 after the reorder, below the 130 to 250 us a lease reader needs to reach its connect, and a caller timed at that band's floor landed in the interval 0 of 80 times on the base, 78 of 80 on the round-2 head and 0 of 80 after the reorder. The trade is that the lease appears later by the prelude's cost, about 160 us at a fresh root (the round-2 head's median less the reorder's; the 292 us was that head's whole interval, of which about 100 us is the bind, the chmod and the rename that still follow the lease), lengthening the interval from the host's start to its lease, the one a kernel reading a foreign host's state mid-start falls into (it recovers an orphan and spawns a second host). That interval was read at about 670 ms on the production road by a separate investigation (about 80 ms kernel-side from the spawn specification's directory to the host's start, then the SDK import and the CLI's spawn); this PR's harness runs the pipe transport, where it reads about 1.2 ms, so the figure is the investigation's, not this PR's measurement, and 160 us is about two hundredths of one percent of it. That interval is queued as its own change and not addressed here. The same round guarded hosts/ in the host's constructor, above the journal (a symlinked hosts/ is refused before host.log, identity.json or a journal segment exist and before any CLI starts, where through round 2 all three were written through the link and a CLI spawned), made the kernel's spawn-wait message name host.stderr beside host.log for that refusal class, put the errno back on the host-crashed row as an integer only, and gave _cli_gone the identity predicate _write_lease uses.

KaTeX (about 86 KB served, 348 KB raw) rode inside every bundle that imports the math grammar: on the fork render.js, feed.js, files.js and waiting.js, so every page that hosts the chat or the viewer downloaded and parsed it whether or not it showed a formula, and the default open (shell, chat, feed) carried two copies. The change makes it an on-demand chunk in the pattern of the editor and PDF chunks. math-chunk.ts is its own webview entry and registers KaTeX as globalThis.__rompKatex. math.ts imports no KaTeX (it types the part it calls by shape). Until the engine has loaded, a formula that passes the fill's bounds stays as its placeholder: the TeX as text, in a dim pending dress both sheets give it, a display formula in KaTeX's display box. It is stamped with its fill call's group, so the per-message budget is still charged per message when it renders later, and the fill asks for the chunk once, through a script tag derived from the page's own bundle tag (chunk-url.ts: the same directory and ?v= token, and the tag's nonce, which the VS Code webview's nonce-only policy requires). When the chunk and the two common KaTeX faces have loaded, the settle handlers run and one fill over the document lays out every waiting formula. The chat keeps the reader's line, or the bottom, around that in-place swap: the first line of text at the viewport top that no formula owns, named by its markdown block in the anchor turn and its character offset there with formula text left out in every form (reading-point.ts), since a reply's formulas usually sit in the turn the reader is partway down and keeping that turn's top let them push the text being read down by their growth; the reload record carries the same line, so a record taken over laid-out formulas lands right over waiting ones, also when the turn is above the fresh page's tail window and the deep-link land's keep offset brings it back (whose window build now puts its fork spots on before the landing, as every sync does); a tab left while a formula waits keeps its reader's line, so a swap that lands while the tab is hidden does not move the text when they come back; the viewer holds a Rendered paint whose block has a waiting formula (the romp loader stays up) and paints once the engine is in, so its first paint is its final one; an open's target (a heading, an offset) waits with the paint and lands at it, never spent over the loader. A load that fails, registers nothing, finds no bundle tag or has not settled within 60 s shows each formula as its source with the reason in its title, logs once, and is not retried until the page reloads.

Measured on the fork (the production webview build in memory, served bytes by the kernel's gzip rule): render.js 573,484 to 489,161 bytes, feed.js 416,874 to 332,224, files.js 303,522 to 218,826, waiting.js 317,751 to 233,157; math-chunk.js 85,680. The default open after a deploy drops from 1,844,286 to 1,675,373 bytes (-168,913, -9.2%); an open that shows math pays for the chunk once.

At upstream/main (f4a572008, 2026-09-24) math.ts imports KaTeX statically and chat-md.ts registers the fill; only render.ts imports chat-md.ts, so only render.js carries KaTeX there, one copy per chat open. The project has no md-config.ts, no waiting.ts and none of the fork's bounds, so an offer would be adapted: the chunk, chunk-url.ts and the engine half of math.ts carry over, the group stamp only matters beside the budget, and the chat's arrival handler and the viewer's hold apply where render.ts and file-view.ts have the same paint paths. Tier feature: it adds an on-demand chunk and a pending state and fixes nothing broken. The editor and PDF loaders' missing nonce and their pattern's omission of waiting.js are separate candidates, not this entry's.

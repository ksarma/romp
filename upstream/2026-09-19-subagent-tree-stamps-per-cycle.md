The agent-file lookup's memo (_subagent_file) re-validated a hit by stat-ing every directory its original walk read, which for a nested or missing agent's file is the whole subagents tree; _awaiting_nest resolves every awaiting agent's file per _session_awaiting call, and the pusher makes up to five such calls per session per cycle, the jobs pass one more. So one call over A nested agents paid (A+1) x D directory stats with nothing changed (two or more agents, or a command row whose owner is read from their transcripts). Measured on two deployed kernels on 2026-09-19: a 20 s py-spy profile of one had _dir_stamp's one os.stat as the top self frame, 28 percent of the samples by the profile's reading, over trees of 305, 383 and 72 directories; the other's memos.subagentTree counters showed 24.5 million validation lstats and 22.7 minutes in validateMs over 6.8 hours across 1,294 directories.

The fix keys the validation on the event a time window would have stood in for: the pusher cycle and the jobs pass. Each opens a thread-confined scope on _live_scope (_subagent_scope_open inside the try beside the other per-cycle slots, closed in the finally) holding three memos: the validated (directories, stats) pair per root, each directory's stamp in the shape _dir_stamp answers, and _awaiting_nest's per-agent launch ids keyed (transcript, agentId). The first reader of a tree in the cycle validates or walks it; every later reader on that thread in the cycle, the agent-file lookup's stamp re-check included, pays no stat. A root leaving _SUBAGENT_TREES moves _SUBAGENT_TREES_GEN and every open scope empties itself before serving, so an eviction is honoured on both threads. Nothing failed is held: a missing or replaced root, a walk with a failed listing or child lstat, a stamp whose stat raises and a launch fold that did not read the file are answered as before and leave no entry. _dir_stamp now counts each successful os.stat into dirStats, so the counter reads both validators (the lstat half alone before), and a figure from before the deploy and one from after are not one series.

Bound per tree per cycle and per pass, for the two loops' own reads: D os.lstat (the one validation) and 0 os.stat when the tree is read before any agent-file lookup, at most D of each when a command row's owner lookup re-checks stamps first, plus the miss path's project and sibling directory stats; one re-validation more per eviction event in the cycle. Stale window: a change on disk after the cycle's validation is seen by the next cycle's first reader, one cycle later at most (the pusher's wake or its 0.5 s backstop under the 1 s minimum interval, the jobs thread's 0.5 s pace, plus the cycle's own duration). Handler-thread builds (WS and HTTP), connect pushes and the act-now nudge pass hold no scope and read per call as before.

tests/test_subagent_tree_stamps_per_cycle.py: a tree of eight directories with three nested workflow agents beside a synthetic session, driven through the real _pusher_cycle and _jobs_cycle with the tick jobs stubbed to three _session_awaiting reads each. Before the fix one cycle paid 72 os.stat on the tree's directories (3 x A x D), 24 os.lstat and dirStats +21; after, 0 os.stat, 8 os.lstat (D), dirStats +7 (D - 1), one hit and 3 agent-file stats. Also: a directory landing between cycles is seen by the next cycle and not by the same one; a pusher cycle and a jobs pass at once each validate once with their own scope; the forget, faulted-stamp, unclean-walk, faulted-launch-fold and vanished-root guards are shown against the input each refuses and accepts. Red under the fix reverted (eleven failures) and under each named broken variant (sticky scope, module-level memo, no gen move from the forget or from the missing-root pop, a held fault, a held unclean walk, a held launch fold that read nothing).

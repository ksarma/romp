A production kernel at 6.8 h up read gc counts [188, 5, 175] against thresholds [700, 10, 10]: 701 full collections in 6.8 h (103 an hour, one every 35 s, mean 2.97 s, last 4.32 s, max 5.70 s, 2,082 s in all, 8.5% of a core), each holding the interpreter lock so every page and request stalled with it. CPython runs a full collection only when counts[2] exceeds thresholds[2] AND the objects promoted since the last one exceed a quarter of the long-lived total; counts[2] rises once per generation-1 collection, so at 175 against 10 the count gate was permanently open and the quarter rule alone timed full collections. The kernel now sets the third threshold once at boot, beside install_gc_hook, leaving the first two as CPython set them; the collector's own generation-1 count (136,738 in 24,480 s, 5.6 a second) then spaces full collections at least 1,001 generation-1 collections apart, at that rate at least 179 s, at most about 20 an hour against 103, each pause about the same length. Nothing reads a clock.

The knob ROMP_GC_GEN2_THRESHOLD: unset or empty applies the default 1,000 (the measured generation-1 rate times a 180 s gap, 1,006, rounded); 0 leaves CPython's own thresholds and says nothing; an integer from 1 to 2,147,483,647 (the C int gc.set_threshold takes) applies that value; anything else is said once on stderr, a line prefixed romp-kernel: gc gen2 threshold: naming the knob, the raw value and the default, and read as the default.

The gate is a pure function of build facts. Applied on CPython 3.10 to 3.13 and 3.14.5 up, where a probe measured the third threshold binding the full-collection rate (a kernel-shaped churn ran 12 full collections per 132 generation-1 ones under 10 and none under 1,000). Declined with one stderr line and the thresholds left in place on the free-threaded build (its collector is not generational; the value is stored and never consulted, the same with the GIL re-enabled, so the gate reads sys.abiflags), on CPython 3.14.0 to 3.14.4 (the incremental collector; the value is not stored and reads back 0), off CPython, and outside 3.10 to 3.14. A read-back post-condition catches an unknown collector that does not store the value. The step never raises and is called from main alone, so a test process that loads the module under a private name keeps its own collector's thresholds.

tests/test_gc_gen2_threshold.py pins the step on every build the suite runs, asserting the branch from the thresholds read back: exactly [t0, t1, 1000] with the first two as found, idempotent, N rather than at least N; the knob values, the malformed line asserted by structure; the reason table, a forced decline and this build's own branch; the read-back and never-raises guards through a collector stub; main as the only call site, after install_gc_hook and before the boot warm; no clock in the step; the docs paragraph; and the exact stderr line from a child interpreter. With the gc.set_threshold line removed, 8 of the 21 are red on 3.12; against the kernel before the change, 20 of 21.

Field check on /perf's existing keys after a deploy (no new key): heap.gc.thresholds and gc.thresholds read [700, 10, 1000]; gc.gen.2.collections over uptime_s at or under about 20 an hour against 103; gc.gen.2.msSum per hour at or under about 60 s against 306; msLast, msMax and collectedLast per pass for the pause length, which now also covers the cyclic garbage that waits up to five times longer between full collections; allocatedBlocks and rss_kb between full collections for that garbage's footprint.

A production kernel at 6.8 h up read gc counts [188, 5, 175] against thresholds [700, 10, 10]: 701 full collections in 6.8 h (103 an hour, one every 35 s, mean 2.97 s, last 4.32 s, max 5.70 s, 2,082 s in all, 8.5% of a core), each holding the interpreter lock so every page and request stalled with it. CPython runs a full collection only when counts[2] exceeds thresholds[2] AND the objects promoted since the last one exceed a quarter of the long-lived total; counts[2] rises once per generation-1 collection, so at 175 against 10 the count gate was permanently open and the quarter rule alone timed full collections. The kernel now sets the third threshold once at boot, beside install_gc_hook, leaving the first two as CPython set them; the collector's own generation-1 count (136,738 in 24,480 s, 5.6 a second) then spaces full collections at least 1,001 generation-1 collections apart, at that rate at least 179 s, at most about 20 an hour against 103. Nothing reads a clock.

Measured on 2026-10-06 and 2026-10-07. Two bench kernels over a copy of a production state directory, side by side at matched load and swapping settings halfway, 2 h per setting, one calling this step verbatim: full collections fell from 39.4 to 17.0 an hour and generation-2 time from 140.5 to 60.7 s an hour (57% less; 52 to 60% per paired half hour), push cycles over 2 s from 19.5 to 4.0 per 30 minutes, and the pause did not change (median 3.52 s under 10 and 3.57 s under 1,000, max 4.73 s and 4.43 s). The production kernel ran 7.9 to 8.6 generation-1 collections a second, over a 59.7 h life and over the first 6 h after its restart, so the step bounds full collections at 28 to 31 an hour there against the 47 to 81 it ran without it (40 to 64% fewer; 34% at the 44.7 an hour of that life's last 2.5 h), and cyclic garbage waits 1.6 to 2.7 times as long between them.

The knob ROMP_GC_GEN2_THRESHOLD: unset or empty applies the default 1,000 (the measured generation-1 rate times a 180 s gap, 1,006, rounded); 0 leaves CPython's own thresholds and says nothing; an integer from 1 to 2,147,483,647 (the C int gc.set_threshold takes) applies that value; anything else is said once on stderr, a line prefixed romp-kernel: gc gen2 threshold: naming the knob, the raw value and the default, and read as the default.

The gate is a pure function of build facts. Applied on CPython 3.10 to 3.13 and 3.14.5 up, where a probe measured the third threshold binding the full-collection rate (a kernel-shaped churn ran 12 full collections per 132 generation-1 ones under 10 and none under 1,000). Declined with one stderr line and the thresholds left in place on the free-threaded build (its collector is not generational; the value is stored and never consulted, the same with the GIL re-enabled, so the gate reads sys.abiflags), on CPython 3.14.0 to 3.14.4 (the incremental collector; the value is not stored and reads back 0), off CPython, and outside 3.10 to 3.14. A read-back post-condition catches an unknown collector that does not store the value. The step never raises and is called from main alone, so a test process that loads the module under a private name keeps its own collector's thresholds.

tests/test_gc_gen2_threshold.py pins the step on every build the suite runs: exactly [t0, t1, 1000] with the first two as found, idempotent, N rather than at least N, an explicit knob value applied and the default applied under a malformed one, through a storing collector stub with the gate opened, and again on the real collector where the step applies, the branch asserted from the thresholds read back; the knob values, the malformed line asserted by structure; the reason table, a forced decline, a malformed knob and a decline each said on a line of its own, and this build's own branch; the read-back and never-raises guards through a collector stub, and the stderr guard through a stderr whose writes raise; main as the only call site, after install_gc_hook and before the boot warm; no clock in the step; the docs paragraph; and the exact stderr line from a child interpreter. With the gc.set_threshold line removed, 12 of the 29 are red on 3.12 and 4 on the free-threaded 3.14 build; against the kernel before the change, 28 of 29 (both counted on 2026-10-07, after the merge with fork main).

Field check on /perf's existing keys after a deploy (no new key): heap.gc.thresholds and gc.thresholds read [700, 10, 1000]; gc.gen.2.collections at or under gc.gen.1.collections over 1,001 for the same span, 28 to 31 an hour at the measured generation-1 rate; gc.gen.2.msSum per hour 34 to 64% under the kernel's rate before the deploy (about 93 to 180 s an hour against 229 to 293 there); msLast, msMax and collectedLast per pass for the pause length, which now also covers the cyclic garbage that waits 1.6 to 2.7 times as long between full collections; allocatedBlocks and rss_kb between full collections for that garbage's footprint.

Upstream's public main (read 2026-10-07; its tip unchanged since 2026-09-24) carries kernel/gc_freeze.py, which landed there on 2026-09-22 (UTC) for its issue 1735 and is on by default (ROMP_GC_FREEZE=off turns it off): at the pusher's idle boundary it runs gc.collect() then gc.freeze(), so a later full collection walks only what was allocated since, and /perf's gc block there carries a freeze sub-block. It sets no threshold, and neither does upstream's kernel/kernel.py (no set_threshold under its kernel/), so this step collides with nothing there. The two compose: thresholds govern only the automatic collections; the freeze's explicit collections run regardless, and a full one zeroes the generation-2 count (probed on 3.10 and 3.12: counts [0, 0, 0] after gc.collect(), the third threshold still 1000). On upstream's main the step would space the automatic full collections between reconciles, each walking only the unfrozen objects; the figures above were measured without the freeze, so what the step adds there is unmeasured. Upstream's main still calls install_gc_hook early in main, as its third statement (after the ROMP_CLAUDE_BIN default and the SIGTERM handler, before the credentials check), and that call is the step's anchor; its reference's gc bullet now runs on through the freeze sub-block, so an offered paragraph goes after that text.
